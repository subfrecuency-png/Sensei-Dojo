Tool Bench

Six working instruments. Change the inputs and watch where the hinges are. Every figure is sourced in the Evidence Base.

1 · S-Corp Election Simulator

Most calculators show payroll-tax savings and stop. This one also prices what the election costs you in QBI deduction — the term that flips the answer more often than people expect.

2 · Depreciation Engine — §179 & 100% Bonus

2026: §179 capped at $2,560,000, phasing out dollar-for-dollar above $4,090,000 (IRC 179(b); Rev. Proc. 2025-32). 100% bonus is permanent for property acquired after 19 Jan 2025 (IRC 168(k); OBBBA sec. 70301).

3 · QBI (§199A) Estimator

Made permanent by OBBBA sec. 70105. Phase-in widened to $75,000 / $150,000 for 2026, with a new $400 floor where QBI is at least $1,000.

4 · SBA Loan Cliff Finder

SBA 7(a) fees and rate caps step at exact dollar amounts. Loan sizing is normally treated as "how much do I need" — it should also be "which side of the cliff am I on." FY2026 fee schedule, SBA Information Notice 5000-872051.

FY2026 runs through 30 Sept 2026. The manufacturer fee waiver applies to loans of $950,000 or less and is set annually by SBA notice — it may not renew.

5 · Credit Ladder Diagnostic

Tiers cannot be skipped. Check only what is genuinely true today; the diagnostic finds your rung and hands you the next three moves.

6 · Funding Readiness Scorecard

What an underwriter sees when your file crosses the desk. Weighted, because not every line carries equal weight.

Every tool here is a simplified teaching model. Real returns involve state tax, NIIT, AMT, basis, at-risk and passive activity rules, and facts these inputs never asked about. Use them to understand the shape of a decision, then take the decision itself to a professional.

Red Flags

Claims that failed scrutiny, and arrangements the IRS has formally designated abusive. These are not grey areas — each is contradicted by a primary source. They appear here because they are actively marketed, often alongside legitimate planning.

Two tells worth memorising. First, any pitch citing a private letter ruling as authority — under IRC 6110(k)(3) a PLR may not be used or cited as precedent by anyone other than the taxpayer who requested it. Second, any arrangement sold on contingency as a percentage of tax saved, which aligns the promoter with the size of the deduction rather than its defensibility.

Participating in a listed transaction without filing Form 8886 carries a penalty up to $100,000 for an individual and $200,000 for an entity under IRC 6707A — whether or not the underlying position is ultimately sustained. IRC 7701(o) codifies the economic substance doctrine, with a 40% strict-liability penalty under IRC 6662(i) if undisclosed and no reasonable-cause defense.

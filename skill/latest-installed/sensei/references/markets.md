## Markets — what the return record actually shows

Where the student asks what to do with retained earnings, or brings you a manager, a fund, or a strategy.

- **[B] Active management underperforms, and the failure rate rises with horizon.** Over the 20 years to end-2024, **94.1%** of US domestic funds underperformed the S&P Composite 1500 — **97.3%** on a risk-adjusted basis. Of 18 domestic categories only two had fewer than 90% underperforming; large value was "best" at 87.8%. *S&P Dow Jones Indices SPIVA — published methodology, near-census of the fund universe.* Graded B, not A: the methodology has published critiques on benchmark selection and share-class treatment. **The direction is robust across methods; the decimals are not.**
- **[B] Cost is the most reliable predictor of future fund returns tested — better than past performance.** Cheapest quintile against priciest: US equity 62% vs 20% success, international equity 51% vs 21%, taxable bond 59% vs 17%. *Morningstar fee studies.* Note the admission against interest — the finding says fees beat the publisher's own star ratings.
- **[A] The arithmetic, so the student can check it.** $100,000 at 7% gross over 30 years: at 0.03% it becomes $754,849; at 1.00%, $574,349; at 2.00%, **$432,194**. **A 2% annual fee consumes 43% of terminal wealth**, and none of it is contingent on performance.
- **[B] Frequent trading destroys returns — replicated across three markets and three decades.** US: the most active quintile of 66,465 households returned 11.4% against a 17.9% market, a **6.5-point annual drag** (*Barber & Odean, 2000*). Taiwan: across **3.7 billion transactions**, day traders lost **23.9bp per day net of fees**, aggregate performance was reliably negative in **14 of 15 years**, and consistently profitable day traders were about **5%** of active traders. Brazil equity futures: of those persisting beyond 300 days, **97% lost money** and 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage. **One direction, three independent administrative datasets.**
- **[A] Most published strategy research does not replicate.** Of **447** tested anomalies, **64% were insignificant at the 5% level**; applying the t > 3.0 threshold proposed for a literature that had already published **316 factors**, **85% were insignificant**. *Hou, Xue & Zhang, "Replicating Anomalies"; Harvey, Liu & Zhu, RFS 2016.* Report the dispute honestly — *Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen, Journal of Finance 2023* reach a more optimistic conclusion on a Bayesian hierarchical framework. **What is not disputed is that searching thousands of specifications manufactures false positives at scale.** A backtest with no out-of-sample period, no specification count and no transaction costs is tier D whatever its Sharpe ratio.
- **[C] A long-run compound return can be almost entirely one year. Check before treating it as a process.** Calendar-year returns 2015 through July 2026 — commercial series with disclosed methodology, cross-checked against a second series; underlying exchange prices are not independently auditable. The arithmetic below is computed, not quoted:

| | Bitcoin | Ethereum | S&P 500 total return |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $10,000 becomes | $2,070,803 | $59,989 | $44,013 |
| **Compound annual rate** | **55.96%** | **19.62%** | **13.14%** |
| Arithmetic mean year | 174.19% | 94.21% | 14.12% |
| **Median year** | **75.94%** | **21.62%** | **18.14%** |
| Up years / down years | 8 / 4 | **5 / 5** | 10 / 2 |
| **$10,000 removing the best year** | **$140,976** | **$10,538** | $33,473 |
| **Share of result from one year** | **93.2%** | **82.4%** | 23.9% |

  **93.2% of Bitcoin's entire eleven-and-a-half-year result came from 2017 alone.** For Ethereum, 82.4% came from 2020 — strip that year and a decade of holding returned **5.4% in total**, on an asset up in five years and down in five. **Neither headline compound rate describes a repeatable process.** Note also how far the arithmetic mean sits above both the median and the compound rate. For volatile assets the mean is the number no holder ever received, and it is the one most often quoted at you.
- **[A] The σ²/2 volatility-drag shortcut fails at high volatility — Sensei had this wrong before testing it.** The approximation estimates the gap between arithmetic mean and compound return well for equities: 1.02 points predicted against 0.98 observed for the S&P 500. For Bitcoin it predicts roughly **700 points against 118 observed** — wrong by a factor of six. **The drag is real and enormous; the textbook shortcut for estimating it is not valid at that scale.** Use observed arithmetic minus geometric. *A master who will not correct himself in front of the student teaches the wrong lesson twice.*
- **[A] Drawdown recovery is non-linear, and this is pure arithmetic:** −20% needs **+25%** · −50% needs **+100%** · −77% needs **+335%** · −84% needs **+525%** · −93% needs **+1,329%**. The same curve governs a leveraged property that loses equity.
- **[C] Correlation estimated from a decade of annual returns is worthless, and is quoted constantly.** Computed 2015–2026: Bitcoin against the S&P 500 gives 0.318; Bitcoin against Ethereum gives 0.271. **Do not use either.** With ten to twelve observations the standard error swamps the estimate — and a 0.27 correlation between Bitcoin and Ethereum is self-evidently wrong as a description of two assets that move together. The annual frequency destroyed the information. **This is "who is missing from the sample" wearing a different hat: what is missing is the observations.**
- **[C] A cyclical pattern with n = 3 is a pattern, not a distribution.** Bitcoin's halving-to-peak interval has run **17, 18 and 18 months**; peak-to-trough has run **12 and 12**. Tight — and built on three observations and two respectively, with no established causal mechanism, promoted overwhelmingly by holders, and **already diverging**: at month nine past the peak, prior cycles were down roughly 65% and 70% while the current one is down 47%. The market also changed structurally in between — spot ETFs from January 2024, generic listing standards from September 2025, fair-value accounting from FY2025, federal stablecoin law from July 2025, tax reporting from January 2026. **A model fitted before those breaks is out of sample now.** And the next halving date is itself an estimate: halvings occur at a block height, not a calendar date, and published 2028 estimates span 26 March to 7 May.
- **[B] Reported crypto exchange volume is contaminated.** Wash trading averaged **over 70%** of reported volume on unregulated exchanges — roughly 77.5% on average, over 81.7% on unregulated Tier-2 venues, and up to 53.4% on some Tier-1 venues; an estimated $4.5tn of spot and $1.5tn of derivatives volume in Q1 2020 alone. *Cong, Li, Tang & Yang, "Crypto Wash Trading," NBER w30783; Management Science, 2023.* **Every metric derived from reported volume inherits this — liquidity rankings, "most traded" lists, volume-weighted indices. On-chain settlement data is materially more trustworthy because it is externally verifiable.**
- **[D] Roughly 53% of the ~20.2 million tokens listed between mid-2021 and end-2025 are no longer actively traded**, with 11.6 million dying in 2025 alone. *CoinGecko / GeckoTerminal — commercial research, but with disclosed methodology, which is more than most.* Dominated by launchpad memecoins, so it says little about assets with meaningful capitalisation. **What it does establish: the modal token is worthless, and any strategy sampling from "new tokens" draws from that distribution.**

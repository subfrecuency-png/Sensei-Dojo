## Real property — the tax engine

Real estate is not a high-return asset class unlevered and pre-tax. It is a **tax-advantaged** one, and nearly all of the advantage sits in a handful of code sections. Everything below is federal, tax year 2026, post-OBBBA (P.L. 119-21, enacted 4 July 2025).

- **[A] 100% bonus depreciation is permanent again.** OBBBA restored it for property acquired after 19 Jan 2025 and placed in service on or after 20 Jan 2025, and repealed the TCJA phase-down. Qualifying property is generally MACRS recovery period **20 years or less** — which is exactly what a cost segregation study produces. **Any advice still using phase-out urgency predates July 2025.** *IRC 168(k)*
- **[A] MACRS lives:** 27.5 residential rental · 39 nonresidential · 15 land improvements and qualified improvement property · 5 and 7 personal property · **land never**. Land allocation therefore controls the deduction; an appraisal split is more defensible than the assessor's ratio, which is common practice and **not** a safe harbour. *IRC 168*
- **[A] New IRC 168(n)** allows elective 100% expensing of the *building shell* for nonresidential real property used in US manufacturing, production or refining. First time in modern law the structure itself is expensable. Narrow, and transformative for an industrial owner-user.
- **[A] Section 179 reaches roofs, HVAC, fire protection and security on nonresidential property placed in service after the building.** Bonus does not. But 179 cannot create a loss and bonus can. **Order it: 179 first for what bonus cannot reach, bonus for the rest.** 179 is generally unavailable for residential rental. *IRC 179(d), (f)*
- **[C] Cost segregation typically reclassifies 15–35% of improvement basis** into 20-year-or-less classes; 15–25% is typical for plain apartment or office. No government dataset publishes this. **A percentage promised before the study is a sales number, not a finding.**
- **[A] A look-back study needs no amended returns** — cumulative catch-up as a 481(a) adjustment on Form 3115, automatic method change. The most under-used move available to anyone who has held property 3–10 years. *Rev. Proc. 2015-13*
- **[A] Then the bill arrives. Section 1245 recapture is ordinary income with no cap** — everything a cost seg moved to 5- and 7-year life comes back at up to 37% plus state plus NIIT. Straight-line depreciation on the shell returns as **unrecaptured 1250 gain capped at 25%**. Fifteen-year land improvements are 1250, not 1245; **many popular sources say otherwise and are wrong.** *IRC 1245, 1250*
- **[A] Recapture is not deferrable by installment sale.** 453(i) requires it recognised in full in the year of disposition even if you receive no cash. On a seller-financed exit of cost-segregated property the tax can exceed the down payment. Reg. 1.453-12 also recognises unrecaptured 1250 gain **first** as payments arrive. *IRC 453(i)*
- **[A] Section 453A charges interest on the deferral** where sale price exceeds $150,000 and aggregate outstanding installment obligations at year end exceed $5,000,000.
- **[A] Section 1031 is intact and unchanged after OBBBA.** The $500,000 annual cap proposal did not pass. Real property only since TCJA. **45 days to identify in writing to the qualified intermediary; 180 days to close OR the return due date including extensions, whichever is earlier** — a Q4 relinquishment silently shortens to April unless you extend, so extend. Boot: **you must replace both the equity and the debt.** *IRC 1031*
- **[A] Reverse and improvement exchanges have a safe harbour** — an exchange accommodation titleholder parks title, same 45/180 clocks from parking. *Rev. Proc. 2000-37*
- **[A] A Delaware Statutory Trust interest is like-kind real property** *(Rev. Rul. 2004-86)* — the standard answer when 45 days will not produce a replacement. Price the constraint honestly: the seven prohibitions forbid new capital, refinancing, re-leasing except on tenant insolvency, reinvestment and more than minor improvements, and require quarterly distribution. **A DST is legally unable to adapt.** A partnership interest is **not** like-kind — syndication LP units cannot be exchanged.
- **[A] Section 121 excludes $250,000 / $500,000, and has not been indexed since 1997.** Own and occupy as principal residence 2 of the last 5 years. **The exception that makes the strategy work:** periods *after* the last date it was your principal residence are not nonqualified use — live in it two years, move out, rent up to three more, sell inside the window, keep the full exclusion. **The reverse order does not work.** Depreciation *allowed or allowable* after 6 May 1997 is never excluded. *IRC 121(b)(5), (d)(6)*
- **[A] Section 469 decides whether your paper losses are worth anything, and most people clear none of the three doors.**
  - **The $25,000 allowance** — active participation, phased out $100,000–$150,000 MAGI. Written in 1986, never indexed. Effectively closed to anyone earning enough to want it. *469(i)*
  - **Real estate professional status** — more than 50% of *all* personal services **and** more than 750 hours in real property trades or businesses, plus material participation per activity unless you file the 1.469-9(g) aggregation election. **A real estate licence does not confer it.** A full-time W-2 job makes the 50% test arithmetically brutal. The failure mode is documentation; courts reject reconstructed logs. *469(c)(7)*
  - **The short-term rental exception** — average period of customer use **7 days or less** means the activity is **not a rental activity at all**, so the per-se passive rule never applies and ordinary material participation decides. **REPS is not required.** Average is total rental days divided by number of separate stays, recomputed every December because it creeps. *Reg. 1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii)(A)*
- **[A] Self-rental is asymmetric by design:** net income from property leased to a business you materially participate in is recharacterised **nonpassive**, while net losses stay **passive** and suspend. The fix is the **1.469-4 grouping election** — in writing, binding absent a material change in facts. *Reg. 1.469-2(f)(6)*
- **[A] Hotel-like services cost you the rental exclusion from self-employment tax.** Daily cleaning during a stay, linens, meals, concierge, transport push you to Schedule C and 15.3%. The tension is real, because those same services help the 30-day exception. *IRC 1402(a)(1); Reg. 1.1402(a)-4(c)(2)*
- **[A] Section 199A is permanent under OBBBA and rental real estate can qualify.** Safe harbour: separate books and records, **250 hours** of rental services (a property manager's hours count), contemporaneous records. **Triple-net-leased property and taxpayer-residence property are excluded from the safe harbour** — NNN owners must argue 162 trade-or-business status on facts. *Rev. Proc. 2019-38*
- **[A] The 163(j)(7)(B) election-out is now an expensive trade.** Electing out of the interest limitation puts you on ADS — 40 / 30 / 20 years, straight line — and **ADS property is ineligible for bonus depreciation.** Before OBBBA this cost little; with 100% bonus permanent it is a large surrender. **[A] The IRS has opened a retroactive withdrawal of elections made in tax years 2022, 2023 and 2024.** For anyone affected this is the highest-dollar item on the board. Confirm the current procedure with a CPA before the window closes.
- **[A] Section 461(l) is the second gate, after 469.** $256,000 / $512,000 for 2026 — *lower* than 2025 because OBBBA reset the base to $250,000 / $500,000 indexed from 2024, and made the limitation permanent. Excess becomes an NOL carryforward, not a lost deduction, and NOLs are then capped at 80% of taxable income. **A plan to erase a very high W-2 in one year is arithmetic fiction.**
- **[A] Opportunity Zones: two programmes are running at once, and conflating them is expensive.**
  - **OZ 1.0 deferred gains become taxable in the tax year including 31 December 2026** — on the 2026 return filed April 2027 — whether or not the fund has been sold and whether or not it produced a dollar of cash. Taxed on the **lesser of** the original deferred gain **or** the fund's fair market value on 31 Dec 2026, which makes a defensible year-end valuation worth real money. **The 10-year step-up on fund-level appreciation survives** — do not sell a fund near its ten-year mark to pay this tax. Plan the liquidity separately. *IRC 1400Z-2*
  - **OZ 2.0 is permanent, effective 1 January 2027** through 31 Dec 2033, with governors nominating zones under Rev. Proc. 2026-14 (nomination deadlines and the designation end date → `live.md` RP-05). At least **25% of each state's zones must be rural**. **Rural funds receive a 30% basis step-up instead of 10%, and the substantial-improvement test is halved from 100% of basis to 50%.** The halved test is worth more than the step-up in most pro formas — it changes which deals pencil at all.
- **[A] Section 1014 is the actual exit strategy.** Heirs take fair market value at death; all deferred gain **and all recapture** disappear. Combine it with 1031 and the highest-value real estate tax strategy in the code is not clever — exchange indefinitely, never sell, die owning it. Nothing beats a 100% exclusion.
- **[A] The exit stack for a high earner in a high-tax state:** 1245 recapture at ordinary + 3.8% NIIT · unrecaptured 1250 at 25% + 3.8% · appreciation at 20% + 3.8% · state 0–13.3%. **Character, not rate, is the part you control.**
- **[A] Land carries its own rules.**
  - **Section 266** lets you elect *annually* to capitalise property tax, mortgage interest, insurance and other carrying charges into basis on unimproved, unproductive land. Raw land throws off no income, so those deductions are frequently worthless — suspended, SALT-limited, or exceeding available income. This converts a dead deduction into basis. Year by year, category by category. Almost nobody uses it. *Reg. 1.266-1*
  - **Dealer versus investor is decided per property, not per taxpayer.** Dealer property is inventory: ordinary income, self-employment tax, and **no 1031 and no 453**. Frequency of sales and improvement activity carry the most weight. Segregate flip inventory from hold inventory into separate entities with separate documented intent from the first day, not the first audit.
  - **Section 1237** gives a non-dealer capital gain on the **first five lots** from a tract held five years with **no substantial improvements** (more than a 10% lift to a lot's value). From lot six, 5% of each lot's price is ordinary. **The bind: it forbids exactly the roads, water and sewer that make subdividing worth doing.**
  - Land is **not depreciable** — no shelter during the hold — but 1031 works fully, and land is the ideal 453 installment asset precisely because there is no recapture to accelerate.
- **[A] FIRPTA makes the buyer the withholding agent and personally liable.** 0% at $300,000 or less with buyer-residence use; 10% from $300,001 to $1,000,000 on the same test; **15% otherwise — on the gross price, not the gain.** Take a seller non-foreign affidavit at every closing as routine. *IRC 1445*
- **[A] Two energy incentives closed mid-2026:** 179D terminated for projects **beginning construction after 30 June 2026** (up to roughly $5.81 per square foot); 45L terminated for homes **acquired after 30 June 2026** (up to $5,000 per unit). Work that began or closed in time remains eligible — documenting *begin construction* is now the entire question.
- **[A] The 47 rehabilitation credit is 20% of qualified rehabilitation expenditures on a certified historic structure, claimed ratably over five years** at 4% a year from placed-in-service. TCJA repealed the separate 10% pre-1936 credit. Enlargement and new construction do not qualify; five-year recapture applies. **It stacks with LIHTC and Opportunity Zones** — the most reliable large subsidy still fully available for adaptive reuse.
- **[A] LIHTC got materially larger in 2026:** the 9% per-capita multiplier rose $3.00 → **$3.36** (+12%), and the private-activity-bond financing test dropped from **50% to 25%** of aggregate basis for 4% credits. The bond test is the structurally larger change — it roughly halves volume-cap consumption per deal in states that exhaust their allocation annually. *IRC 42*
- **[A] Conservation easements: 170(h)(7) disallows the deduction entirely** where a partnership's or S corporation's contribution exceeds **2.5 times partners' relevant basis**, with narrow exceptions for a three-year holding period, family-owned pass-throughs and certified historic structures. Syndicated versions are **listed transactions**. *T.D. 10007; Notice 2017-10*

## Capital access — debt

- **[A] SBA does not make grants to start or expand a for-profit business.** Federal money reaching a for-profit is almost always SBIR/STTR, sector cost-share (e.g. USDA REAP), or a state/nonprofit pass-through.
- **[A] SBA 7(a) guaranty is 85% at or below $150,000 and 75% above** — not 90%. Max loan $5,000,000; max SBA exposure $3,750,000. **SBA Express is a separate product with a 50% maximum guaranty** — the widely-quoted "50% to 85%" range conflates the two and misstates the economics.
- **[A] The pricing cliffs are arithmetic, and they are large:**
  - **$150,000** — guaranty steps 85% → 75%
  - **$350,000** — max rate spread tightens base + 4.5% → base + 3.0%. A borrower asking $340,000 shops a market 150bp worse than one asking $360,000.
  - **$700,000 / $1,000,000** — upfront fee steps 3.0% → 3.5%, then 3.75% on the guaranteed portion above $1M
- **[A] SBA sets 7(a) and 504 fees annually by Information Notice for each fiscal year (1 October – 30 September); the approval date, not the application date, picks the schedule.** The current schedule, its waivers and ceilings, and the expired FY2026 manufacturer waiver → `live.md` SBA-01.
- **[A] SBA microloans cap at $50,000** and are issued through non-profit intermediaries, which set their own rates — not through banks.
- **[B] SBA lending creates roughly 3 to 3.5 jobs per $1,000,000 lent.** Effects are larger for younger firms and **larger where local credit conditions are weak** — evidence the loans relieve a genuinely binding constraint rather than substituting for available private credit. *Brown & Earle, Journal of Finance 72(3), 2017 — linked administrative data on all SBA loans, lenders and US employers*
- **[A] An SBA loan is not government money.** It is a private lender's loan with a partial federal guarantee. The lender underwrites, prices, approves and collects. **The guarantee protects the lender, not you**, and personal guarantees are standard.
- **[A] Regulation B is a free diagnostic after any decline.** Over $1,000,000 in gross revenues you get written reasons *only if you request them in writing within 60 days*. Below that, notification within 30 days, possibly oral. Almost nobody asks. *12 CFR §1002.9*
- **[A] Small banks fully approved 57% of applicants** — the highest of any lender type. Online lenders drew 29% of applications, and 60% of their borrowers reported costs higher than expected (vs 37% small banks, 32% large banks). Only **42%** of applicants got the full amount sought; **22%** got nothing. Correct sequence: small bank / credit union → CDFI or SBA microlender → large bank → online lender last. *Federal Reserve, 2026 Report on Employer Firms — convenience sample, not random. Treat the ordering as reliable and the decimals as not.*
- **[A] 59% of firms carrying debt used a personal guarantee**; 51% pledged business assets.
- **[C] 1,432 Treasury-certified CDFIs** as of winter 2025; the CDFI Fund awarded 357 institutions $408.2M in FY2024 Financial Assistance. CDFIs are a **different underwriting box**, not a worse-priced version of the same one — they are mandated toward borrowers conventional underwriting declines.
- **[A] Federal thresholds moved 1 October 2025:** micro-purchase $10,000 → **$15,000**; simplified acquisition $250,000 → **$350,000**; subcontracting plan $750,000 → **$900,000**. Under FAR 19.502-2(a) that band is where small-business set-aside is the **default**.
- `[CA]` **[A] California usury: business purpose moves you to a higher cap, it does not remove the cap.** Points and fees count toward the ceiling under the anti-evasion clause. The exemption actually used is the real-estate-broker clause — a loan made **or arranged** by a CA-licensed broker and secured at least in part by real property. *Cal. Const. Art. XV §1*
- `[CA]` **[C] Ten investors is a wall, not a speed bump** for fractionalized trust deeds. *B&P §10229; Corp. Code §25102.5*
- **[C] MCAs are recharacterized as loans** where the contract lacks genuine reconciliation or the provider bears no real risk of loss. FTC obtained a $20.3M judgment against operator Jonathan Braun (Feb 2024).
- `[CA]` **[C] A merchant cash advance's true APR cannot be computed at signing — its own regulator said so.** California SB 1235 (2018) took four years of rulemaking, took effect December 2022, and the DFPI created an **estimated-APR safe harbour** precisely because disclosed and effective APR "may vary substantially" when repayment depends on future sales. Do the arithmetic yourself: (total repayment − advance) ÷ advance, annualised over your realistic repayment period.

## Capital access — equity, grants and the securities container

- **[A] An early-stage SBIR award roughly DOUBLES the probability of subsequently raising venture capital**, with large positive effects on patenting and revenue, and **the largest effects among the most financially constrained firms**. The mechanism is **funding prototyping — not certification or signalling**, so the benefit accrues even if no investor ever learns of the award. *Howell, American Economic Review 107(4), 2017 — regression discontinuity on ranked DOE SBIR applicants, 7,436 firms, $884M in awards.* **This is the highest expected-value capital available to a technical founder, and it costs zero equity.** Eleven federal agencies run SBIR/STTR on differing solicitation cycles.
- **[A] Angel funding causally improves the business — survival, employment, patenting, web traffic, exits.** *Kerr, Lerner & Schoar, Review of Financial Studies 27(1), 2014 — regression discontinuity on angel-group interest thresholds.*
- **[A] But angel funding showed NO effect on access to further financing at the discontinuity.** The value landed in the operations, not in the signal to the next investor. **If the thesis for the round is "it signals Series A," the best evidence available does not support it.**
- **[A] Accelerator cash and co-working space alone showed no measurable effect on venture performance. Only the bundled schooling did.** *Gonzalez-Uribe & Leatherbee, Review of Financial Studies 31(4), 2018 — regression discontinuity on Start-Up Chile.* Evaluate a programme on curriculum. Boundary: studied an ecosystem accelerator taking young early-stage ventures; generalisation to top-tier selective programmes is **not established and cannot be**, because their selectivity is what removes the design.
- **[B] Crowdfunding validates that a crowd will pay — nothing else.** Over **75% of funded Kickstarter projects deliver late, and more funding predicts more delay** (*Mollick, Journal of Business Venturing 29(1), 2014 — 48,500+ projects*). Compared against an expert panel, crowds and experts largely agree; where they diverge, **the crowd funds projects experts would not** — more permissive, not better (*Mollick & Nanda, Management Science 62(6), 2016*). Overfunding is a scaling problem arriving before the capability to absorb it.
- **[C] Exemption caps:** Reg CF **$5,000,000** per 12 months. Reg A Tier 1 **$20,000,000**; Tier 2 **$75,000,000**, with ongoing reporting. Rule 506(b) and 506(c) are **unlimited** in amount. Form D is filed after first sale. Verify current text at sec.gov — caps have been amended more than once.
- **[A] The trap that catches founders: Rule 506(b) prohibits general solicitation.** A public post, open newsletter, livestreamed pitch or podcast describing the offering can constitute general solicitation and **destroy the exemption**, potentially giving investors rescission rights. 506(b) permits unlimited accredited plus **up to 35 non-accredited sophisticated** purchasers.
- **[A] To raise publicly you must be in Rule 506(c) — and then every purchaser must be accredited AND verified by the issuer.** Self-certification is not sufficient under 506(c). *17 CFR 230.506*
- **[C] Accredited investor (17 CFR 230.501):** net worth over **$1,000,000** excluding primary residence, **or** income over **$200,000** individually / **$300,000** jointly in each of the two most recent years with reasonable expectation of the same; **or**, since the 2020 amendments, certain professional certifications and knowledgeable employees of the fund. **Nominal thresholds are unchanged as of 2026** — the income test dates to 1982 and the net-worth test to the 2011 primary-residence exclusion, so in real terms they now reach far more households than when written. Dodd-Frank requires SEC review every four years; reviews were completed 2015, 2019, 2023.
- **[D] Venture return concentration is real in direction and unverifiable in magnitude.** Every precise figure — "5% of investments generate 50%+ of returns" — comes from proprietary datasets with undisclosed survivorship. **The part that matters to a founder survives regardless: a fund needing one investment to return the whole fund is structurally required to push every portfolio company toward an outlier outcome.** That incentive may not match a business that would be excellent at moderate scale. Understand it before taking the money, not after.
- **[D] Demographic funding shares** (≈1% of US venture to Black-founded companies, ≈1.9% to all-women teams) come from commercial deal databases with incomplete private-deal coverage and partly inferred tagging. **Direction robust across sources; the decimals are not.** The stronger evidence concerns mechanism: the gap narrows substantially when a Black partner leads the investment team, and such partners rely **less** on observable credentialing proxies — consistent with screening on proxies rather than on venture quality.
- **The routing that follows from the above:** SBIR allocates by **technical review of a written proposal** (tier A evidence of effect). SBA-guaranteed lending allocates by **financial underwriting** (tier B). CDFI lending is **mandated toward underserved borrowers** (tier C documentation). For any founder disadvantaged by network-based screening, these are not consolation prizes — **two of the three carry stronger causal evidence than venture capital does.**

## Counterparty verification — what "audited" and "regulated" actually mean

The highest-yield question Sensei can teach. It applies to a custodian, a lender, a processor, a fund, a sponsor, a property manager and a vendor alike.

- **[C] "Audited" is four different things, and vendors use them interchangeably.** Ranked by assurance:
  1. **Financial statement audit** (PCAOB/ISA) — an opinion that statements are fairly presented in all material respects. Covers assets **and liabilities**. The auditor carries legal liability.
  2. **Examination / attestation** (ISAE 3000) — an opinion on a specific subject matter against stated criteria. Real assurance, narrower scope. Read the criteria.
  3. **Agreed-upon procedures** — the practitioner performs steps *the client selected* and reports findings. **No opinion. No assurance.** Nearly worthless alone.
  4. **Self-attestation** — an assertion wearing a noun.
  **Four questions settle it: audited by whom, under what standard, of what subject matter, and may I read the opinion?** A missing answer to any one of the four *is* the answer. The same four questions apply to a syndicator's "audited" operating statements.
- **[B] "Proof of reserves" is not proof of solvency.** The Merkle-tree construction genuinely proves that stated addresses held stated amounts at a moment, and that your balance sat in the hashed liability set. It does **not** prove liabilities; does not prevent assets being **borrowed for the snapshot and returned after**; **cannot detect accounts omitted from the tree** — your inclusion proof says nothing about who else was left out; and does not prove exclusive key control. **Solvency is reserves minus liabilities. A reserves-only attestation shows one term.** Proof of solvency requires proof of liabilities alongside it, and zero-knowledge constructions for it exist. Verify which one a counterparty actually publishes rather than which one they name.
- **[A] "Licensed" and "registered" mean an entity met entry conditions and is subject to examination.** They do not mean solvent, honest, or backstopped. **Verify the claim itself, free, in minutes:** NMLS Consumer Access (money transmission and mortgage origination), FINRA BrokerCheck and SEC IAPD (securities), FDIC BankFind (bank status), NCUA (credit unions), SEC EDGAR (sworn filings), the state secretary of state (entity standing), and the county recorder (who actually owns the property and what is against it). **A firm claiming regulation that appears in none of these has told you something.**
- **[C] A blockchain proves transactions, not identities.** The ledger is high-grade evidence of what transfers occurred. Who controlled the keys is an **inference layer** built on clustering heuristics and off-chain attribution. In *United States v. Sterlingov* one vendor's methodology survived a Daubert challenge and was admitted — a real result, but provider-specific, with clustering parameters held as trade secrets and the court expressly preserving factual challenge before the jury. **Admissible is not the same as accurate.**

## Base rates — the numbers that reframe the rest

- **[C] Roughly half of new US employer establishments survive five years.** BLS Business Employment Dynamics, a near-census of administrative unemployment-insurance records: **77–84%** survive 1 year, **55–63%** survive 3, **42–50%** survive 5. Recession-birth cohorts fare worst; those surviving four years do materially better thereafter. **"90% of startups fail" is not true of businesses generally and has no primary source.**
- **[C] Most businesses are funded by their founders.** Of US employer businesses: **personal and family savings 63.9%**, bank business loans **17.9%**, personal credit cards **10.3%**. About 90% need some startup capital and roughly two-thirds get it from savings. *Kauffman Foundation calculations from Census Annual Survey of Entrepreneurs.* **Venture capital, angels and accelerators describe an extreme minority path** — most published "startup funding" advice is written about someone else.
- **The concentration test belongs here too.** See *Markets* above: 93.2% of one widely-cited eleven-year compound return came from a single calendar year. **Whenever a rate is offered as evidence of a process, ask what survives deleting the best period.** The same question exposes a customer list carried by one account, a channel carried by one campaign, and a portfolio carried by one property.

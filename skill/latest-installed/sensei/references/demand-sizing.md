## Demand — what advertising and marketing actually do

- **[A] Mean short-term advertising elasticity is 0.12; long-term 0.24.** A 1% increase in advertising spend buys about **0.12%** more sales in the short run. To move sales 10% short-term on advertising alone, the average brand roughly doubles its budget. Elasticity runs higher for durables, early life-cycle, and yearly-measured data. *Sethuraman, Tellis & Briesch, Journal of Marketing Research 48(3), 2011 — meta-analysis of 751 short-term and 402 long-term elasticities across 56 studies.* **Any pitch promising an order of magnitude more is claiming the far tail of a well-measured distribution. Make them show why.**
- **[A] Most firms cannot measure advertising ROI at all.** Across 25 large digital field experiments totalling $2.8M in spend, the **median ROI confidence interval was over 100 percentage points wide**; individual sales carry a coefficient of variation near 10; an informative experiment can require **over 10 million person-weeks**. *Lewis & Rao, Quarterly Journal of Economics 130(4), 2015.* **This is not a reason to despair — it is a reason to stop reporting attributed ROAS as if it were ROI, and to plan on prior evidence instead.**
- **[A] Branded search ads are frequently not incremental.** In randomized experiments at eBay, brand-keyword ads produced **no measurable short-term benefit — about 99.5% of the attributed traffic would have arrived anyway** — and non-brand search returned negative average returns because unaffected frequent users consumed most of the spend. *Blake, Nosko & Tadelis, Econometrica 83(1), 2015.* Boundary: eBay had enormous awareness and strong organic presence. This does not automatically generalise to a small brand — **but it moves the burden of proof onto the spend.**
- **[A] Attribution is not measurement.** Across 15 Facebook RCTs — 500 million user-experiment observations, 1.6 billion impressions — observational methods **frequently failed to recover the experimental effect even with far richer data than any advertiser has.** *Gordon, Zettelmeyer, Bhargava & Chapsky, Marketing Science 38(2), 2019.* Platform-reported conversions and multi-touch attribution are allocation heuristics. Only randomized holdouts, geo-experiments and **ghost-ad designs** produce a defensible incrementality claim. *Johnson, Lewis & Nubbemeyer, JMR 54(6), 2017.*
- **[B] Brand growth comes from more buyers, not more loyalty.** Double jeopardy — smaller brands have both fewer buyers and slightly lower loyalty — replicates across 50+ categories and 30+ countries, including B2B. Loyalty is largely a **consequence** of size. *Ehrenberg, Goodhardt & Barwise, Journal of Marketing, 1990, and subsequent replication.* Caveat honestly: this describes how growth has historically decomposed; it does not by itself settle whether retention spend has better marginal return **in a subscription business with negative churn**.
- **[A] Word of mouth outperforms advertising per unit of input.** eWOM **volume elasticity 0.236, valence elasticity 0.417** — the latter more than triple the advertising elasticity. Higher for privately-consumed, low-trialability goods, and **higher on independent review sites than on retailer-owned ones**. *You, Vadakkepatt & Joshi, Journal of Marketing 79(2), 2015.*
- **[A] A one-star review increase causes a 5–9% revenue increase — for independents. Zero effect for chains.** Regression discontinuity on Yelp rounding thresholds, outcome measured from **state tax records**, not self-report. *Luca, HBS Working Paper 12-016 (2011, rev. 2016) — a working paper, not a peer-reviewed article; the design is what earns the A.* **The asymmetry is the strategic content: reviews substitute for brand information.** If you are unknown, review generation may be your highest-return activity. Once you have recognition, it is hygiene.
- **[A] Review manipulation is detectable and concentrates exactly where the incentive is strongest** — independents facing nearby competitors. *Mayzlin, Dover & Chevalier, American Economic Review 104(8), 2014.* Your competitors' ratings may be inflated; following them is how listings get removed.
- **[A] NPS is not a validated growth predictor.** Tested directly across 21 firms and 15,500+ interviews — including in the industries cited as NPS exemplars — the superiority and best-predictor claims **failed to replicate**. *Keiningham, Cooil, Andreassen & Aksoy, Journal of Marketing 71(3), 2007.* It is a cheap tracking metric. It is not a justification for a decision.
- **[A] Disclosing influencer sponsorship raises recall, costs credibility, and has statistically insignificant net effect on behaviour.** *Meta-analysis, Marketing Letters — 288 effect sizes, 37 studies, n=12,721.* Note honestly that a separate meta-analysis reports a negative disclosure-to-purchase-intention correlation; the field is not unanimous. **Either way, the trade you think you are making by under-disclosing is not supported — and FTC 16 CFR Part 255 (effective 26 July 2023) requires disclosure that is clear, conspicuous, unavoidable in interactive media, and matched to format.**
- **[C] SEO: what is documented and what is not.** Core Web Vitals thresholds are published exactly — LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, CLS ≤0.1, at the **75th percentile** over a **28-day** CrUX window. Google's local model is exactly three documented factors — **relevance, distance, prominence** — with **no published weights**, and ranking cannot be purchased. **Distance is not optimisable**; it is a hard bound on a local business's market and should bound its budget. **[F] "Google has 200 ranking factors"** originated as an offhand estimate around 2009 and was never a published list.
- **[B] AI search is displacing informational clicks, but not by the number people quote.** Pew Research, tracking **900 US adults across 68,879 Google searches** (results collected 7–17 April 2025): users clicked a result on **8%** of visits with an AI summary versus **15%** without, and clicked a source *inside* the summary on about **1%**. **This is not causal** — Google chooses which queries get summaries and favours simple informational ones that already ended without clicks. Vendor figures of 34% or 58% come from undisclosed panels. **What survives: content whose only value is answering a question a model can answer is a depreciating asset.**
- **[B] Generative engine optimisation has exactly one peer-reviewed evidence base.** On a ~10,000-query benchmark, the best methods improved visibility by roughly **41%** on position-adjusted word count and **28%** on subjective impression. The winners were **citing sources, adding quotations, adding statistics**. **Keyword stuffing was among the least effective.** *Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024.* It measures visibility inside generated answers, not traffic or revenue, and it is model-version dependent. **[F] Any "GEO ranking factor" list with weights is fabricated — no model provider publishes citation criteria.**

## Demand — social programs

- **[A] Passive broadcast beats active sharing in aggregate.** Randomized field experiment across **1.4 million friends of 9,687 Facebook users**: passive-broadcast features (automatic visible signal from ordinary use) drove a **246% increase in peer influence and contagion**; active-personalized sharing added only **98%** more and was used far less often, so **passive generated more total adoption**. *Aral & Walker, Management Science 57(9), 2011, 1623–1639.* **Most companies build share buttons — the weaker half of the mechanism. This is a product decision, not a marketing one, and it cannot be retrofitted cheaply.**
- **[A] Weak ties do the heavy lifting.** Randomizing exposure to friends' sharing across **253 million subjects**: exposure causally increased sharing and accelerated it; strong ties are individually more influential, but **weak ties are far more numerous and collectively drive most novel information spread**. *Bakshy, Rosenn, Marlow & Adamic, WWW 2012.* Breadth beats depth when the objective is diffusion.
- **[B] Virality is a distribution, not a strategy.** Across **seven independent online domains**, the vast majority of cascades are small and **terminate within one generation of the seed**. *Goel, Watts & Goldstein, ACM EC 2012; Goel et al., Management Science 2016.* **A plan requiring an outlier draw is not a plan.** The useful corollary: because most diffusion is broadcast-shaped, **initial seed audience size dominates outcomes** far more than any content property. Reach is controllable; depth is not.
- **[B] High-arousal emotion travels; low-arousal does not.** Awe, anger and anxiety increase sharing; sadness decreases it — controlling for surprise, interest, utility and placement. *Berger & Milkman, JMR 49(2), 2012, on the full NYT corpus over three months.* Boundary: this measures sharing of **news articles**, not sales of anything.
- **[A] Early positive signal causally shapes later aggregate signal — asymmetrically.** A single arbitrary first upvote raised final ratings by about **25%** and raised the odds of a subsequent upvote by about **32%**; negative first votes produced **no symmetric effect** — crowds corrected downward manipulation but not upward. *Muchnik, Aral & Taylor, Science 341, 2013, across 100,000+ posts.* **The measured outcome is a rating, not revenue. Seeding your own launch legitimately is sound; reciprocal engagement rings move a metric with no revenue evidence and violate every major platform's terms.**
- **[F] There is no credible evidence on optimal posting time, frequency, hashtag count, or video length.** Every circulating figure is a vendor analysing its own customers. Test on your own account if it matters; expect a small effect; do not pay for someone else's answer.
- **[F] "The algorithm rewards X" is unfalsifiable.** No major social platform publishes ranking criteria. That does not make every such claim false — it makes none of them checkable.
- **[B] Follower count is a lagging indicator, not an asset.** Double jeopardy predicts large brands have more of everything, followers included. Closing a follower gap does not close a share gap.

## Sizing a market without buying a research report

Bottom-up beats top-down. A top-down estimate — "the market is $X billion, we take 1%" — contains no information; the 1% is invented.

```
Addressable units × Purchase frequency × Realistic price × Achievable penetration
```

Every term must trace to a named source or a stated, falsifiable assumption. **[C] The free primary sources:** Census **County Business Patterns** (establishments by NAICS by county — the B2B universe), Census **Economic Census** (revenue by industry, every five years), Census **Business Formation Statistics**, Census **ACS** (tract-level demographics for local radius work), BLS **Consumer Expenditure Survey** (household spending by category and demographic), BLS **Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics** (buyer-population sizing by role and metro), and **SEC EDGAR** for audited competitor figures.

**[C] Google Trends is a normalised 0–100 index from a sampled subset — not absolute volume.** Separately-run queries are not comparable; compare only within one simultaneous query. Low-volume terms are unstable. A systematic review found only ~7% of published Trends analyses were reproducible.

**Before spending on a "trend," name two independent non-attention data series that should move if it is real** — category revenue in the Economic Census, employment in the relevant NAICS code, public-company segment reporting. **If the only evidence is attention data, you have found attention, not a market.**

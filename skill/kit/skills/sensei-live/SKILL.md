# Sensei — the live layer

> **Education, not professional advice.** This file tracks what is in force, pending or expiring under the law of the United States of America, so the student does not mistake a bill for a law or last year's figure for this year's. ## Stablecoins — the GENIUS Act

### Effective date: 18 January 2027 governs — *as of 1 October 2026*

The GENIUS Act (**Pub. L. 119-27**, 139 Stat. 419, approved 18 July 2025) takes effect on the **earlier** of:

1. 18 months after enactment — **18 January 2027**, or
2. 120 days after the primary federal payment stablecoin regulators issue any final implementing regulations.

*Source: Section 20 of the enrolled text, govinfo.gov — read 1 October 2026.*

**Why the direction matters.** Under "earlier of," 18 January 2027 is a **ceiling, not a floor**. Late rules cannot push the date out — they can only fail to pull it in. Anyone told "later of" would plan to be ready *after* January 2027 and would be non-compliant on day one. (Earlier versions of the fact base said "later of". It was wrong.)

**Where rulemaking stands — *as of 1 October 2026*.** Agencies missed the statutory one-year rulemaking target of 18 July 2026 and are still at the proposal stage:

- **Treasury** issued an NPRM on issuance, offer and sale of payment stablecoins on **17 August 2026**; it was published in the Federal Register on **18 August 2026** with a 60-day comment period, so **comments are due 19 October 2026**. *(treasury.gov press release sb0605; federalregister.gov doc. 2026-16796.)*
- The **Federal Reserve Board** requested comment on two issuer proposals (reserves, capital, risk management; application process) on **24 September 2026**, 60 days after Federal Register publication. *(federalreserve.gov press release, 24 September 2026.)*
- Earlier proposals came from the OCC (February 2026), the FDIC and a joint customer-identification proposal (June 2026), and NCUA (June–July 2026).
- No final rule by a *primary* federal payment stablecoin regulator (OCC, Federal Reserve, FDIC, NCUA) appeared in a Federal Register search on 1 October 2026. Treasury published an **interim final rule on 30 September 2026** setting procedures for the Stablecoin Certification Review Committee — Treasury is not a primary regulator under the Act's definition, so on the plain text this does not trigger prong 2.

**So:** a final rule issued on or after 1 October 2026 would trigger a date at least 120 days later — 29 January 2027 or after, which is past the 18 January 2027 backstop. **18 January 2027 is the operative date.** The Act's second date also matters: from **18 July 2028**, digital asset service providers generally may not offer or sell payment stablecoins to persons in the United States unless issued by a licensed issuer *(Treasury's description of the Act, 17 August 2026)*.

Tier **A** on the dates (statutory text). Tier **C** on the rulemaking picture — it moves weekly.

## In force, and recent enough to be missed

### A federal CBDC is barred through 31 December 2030 — *as of 1 October 2026*

The prohibition is **Title XI of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, P.L. 119-101** (H.R. 6644). The Senate agreed to the final House amendment 85–5 on 22 June 2026; the President did not sign it, and it **became law without signature on 11 July 2026**. The Federal Reserve may not issue a CBDC, directly or through an intermediary; open, permissionless, private dollar-denominated currency is carved out. *(congress.gov all-actions listing shows "Became Public Law No: 119-101"; Holland & Knight summary, August 2026.)*

Three traps:

- **It did not arrive on the vehicle people expect.** The Anti-CBDC Surveillance State Act (H.R. 1919) passed the House **219–210** on 17 July 2025 and **is not law**. *(Vote count corrected 1 October 2026 against the sponsor's release and the ABA Banking Journal; an earlier version of this file said 219–217.)* Anyone citing H.R. 1919 as the authority is citing a bill.
- **It sunsets.** 31 December 2030 is a real date, not a permanent settlement.
- **Check the effective date of the title.** One law-firm summary states that several of the Act's titles take effect 7 January 2027. Read the enrolled text before relying on when the CBDC bar became operative. **Unverified** on that point.

Tier **A** on enactment.

### Digital-asset broker reporting is mid-phase-in — *as of 1 October 2026*

- **Gross proceeds** — required for transactions effected on or after **1 January 2025** (first Forms 1099-DA landed early 2026).
- **Cost basis** — required on certain transactions effected on or after **1 January 2026**, in practice **covered assets**: acquired on or after 1 January 2026 *and* held in the same broker's account until sale.

Anything acquired earlier, or transferred in from an outside wallet or another broker, is generally **noncovered** — the broker reports no basis and the taxpayer still owes the calculation. Expect broker-reported basis and personal records to diverge wherever assets moved between venues. Reconcile before filing, not after a notice.

*Authority: Reg. §1.6045-1, final custodial broker regulations (July 2024). Dates re-checked on irs.gov "Digital assets" page (last reviewed 2 September 2026) on 1 October 2026.* Tier **A**.

## SBA fees — FY2027 is now in effect, and it is not a renewal

### The schedule that applies today — *as of 1 October 2026*

SBA published the **FY 2027 fee notices on 3 September 2026**: Information Notice **5000-881797** (7(a)) and **5000-881796** (504). They apply to loans **approved 1 October 2026 through 30 September 2027**. *(Both notices read on sba.gov, 1 October 2026.)*

The FY2026 schedule **ended 30 September 2026**. The manufacturer waiver did not simply continue or lapse — it was replaced by a differently shaped one, **broader in who qualifies, lower in how much qualifies.**

| | FY 2026 (approved through 30 Sep 2026) — expired | FY 2027 (approved 1 Oct 2026 – 30 Sep 2027) — in effect |
|---|---|---|
| Who gets the 7(a) 0% upfront fee | Manufacturers only | Manufacturers (NAICS 31–33), food supply chain, **and** businesses located in a rural area |
| Loan size ceiling for that waiver | **$950,000** | **$700,000** |

A manufacturer with an $800,000 loan paid 0% if approved on 30 September 2026 and pays 3.5% of the guaranteed portion if approved on or after 1 October 2026. **Approval date, not application date, picks the schedule.**

### FY 2027 7(a) fees in full — *as of 1 October 2026; expires 30 September 2027*

- **Lender's Annual Service Fee:** 0.55% of the outstanding balance of the guaranteed portion, including WCP loans. **Lenders may not pass this to the borrower.**
- **Upfront (guaranty) fee, 0%** for loans of **$700,000 or less** to manufacturers (NAICS sectors 31–33), food supply chain (NAICS 1111, 1112, 1113, 1121–1125, 1129, 1141, 1151, 1152, 423820, 4244, 4245, 424910, 445110, 484220, 484230, 493120, 493130 — the two trucking codes limited to refrigerated/frozen, farm products, grain and livestock), and businesses located in a rural area.
- **Otherwise, maturity over 12 months** — percentages are of the *guaranteed portion*, size bands on the *gross* approval amount:
  - $150,000 or less — **2%** (lender may retain up to 25%; at least 1.5% remitted to SBA)
  - $150,001 to $700,000 — **3%**
  - $700,001 to $5,000,000 — **3.5%** up to $1,000,000, plus **3.75%** above $1,000,000
- **Maturity of 12 months or less:** 0.25%
- **SBA Express to veteran-owned businesses:** $0 — statutory, 15 U.S.C. §636(a)(31)(G)
- **EWCP:** 0.25% (≤12 mo) · 0.525% (13–24) · 0.80% (25–36)
- **WCP:** 0.25% (≤12 mo) · 0.525% (13–24) · 0.80% (25–36) · 1.075% (37–48) · 1.35% (49–60)

**The 90-day aggregation rule still bites.** Two or more 7(a) loans over 12 months' maturity to the same applicant or its affiliates approved within 90 days are combined for the upfront fee. Splitting loans to avoid fees is prohibited. This is how a borrower lands over the $700,000 line without noticing.

### FY 2027 504 fees — *as of 1 October 2026; expires 30 September 2027*

Manufacturers, food supply chain and rural-area businesses: **the upfront guaranty fee and the annual service fee are waived — the notice sets no dollar cap.** All other 504 loans: upfront 0.50%, annual service 0.203%. 504 Debt Refinance without Expansion (other than loans to manufacturers): upfront 0.50%, annual service 0.204%.

Note the asymmetry: 7(a) caps the waiver at $700,000, **504 does not**. For a qualifying borrower above the cap, that difference alone can decide which program to use.

Tier **A** — published agency notices with stated effective dates.

### One thing here is not verified — *as of 1 October 2026*

Both notices say "located in a **rural area**" without defining it. Practitioner commentary reports SOP 50 10 8.1 defining it as any county the Census Bureau classifies as at least 30% rural — a much lower bar than the USDA test. **I could not confirm that reading from the SOP text on 1 October 2026.** It is the whole ballgame for the newly eligible third group, so the student's lender must confirm it from the SOP before anyone relies on it. Tier **D** until confirmed.

## Crypto market structure — the bill failed

### CLARITY Act: cloture rejected 49–50 — *as of 1 October 2026*

The **CLARITY Act** (H.R. 3633) passed the House in July 2025. A Senate Banking substitute advanced 15–9 on 14 May 2026. On **15 September 2026 the Senate rejected cloture on the motion to proceed, 49–50** — 11 votes short of 60. Practitioners read this as ending the bill's prospects for the rest of this Congress. *(CNBC, 15 September 2026; Troutman Pepper Locke analysis, 25 September 2026.)*

**It is not law, and on the current calendar it will not become law this Congress.** Jurisdiction over digital assets is set by existing statute, agency action and case law — all of which can turn.

### What the agencies are doing instead — *as of 1 October 2026*

- **SEC:** a Commission **interpretation** on applying the securities laws to certain crypto assets (published 23 March 2026, Federal Register doc. 2026-05635), and a **proposed** "Regulation Crypto Assets" (18 August 2026) with tiered fundraising exemptions and a safe harbor; comments due **20 October 2026**. A proposal binds nothing; an interpretation can be revised by a later Commission.
- **CFTC:** staff no-action letters, updated FAQs, and a pre-rule action sent to OMB review on 17 September 2026. A binding rule is not expected before late 2027 *(practitioner estimate)*. Without new legislation the CFTC's authority over ordinary spot trading is limited to fraud and manipulation enforcement.

Tier **A** on the negative claim — CLARITY is not law. Tier **C** on the agency picture.

### Standing rule for this whole area

Passing one chamber is not law. Advancing out of committee is not law. Filing cloture is not a vote, and a failed cloture vote is not a vote on the bill. An executive order binds the executive branch and can be revoked by the next one. An agency **proposal**, a no-action letter, an interpretation and an "innovation exemption" are agency positions, not statute — and a notice of proposed rulemaking is not a rule. Grade accordingly, and say which one you're looking at.

## How to use this file

1. **Check here first** for anything touching stablecoins, CBDCs, crypto market structure, digital-asset tax reporting, or SBA fees.
2. **State what is in force today**, not what is enacted-but-dormant. "Enacted 18 July 2025, effective 18 January 2027" is the honest form; "the GENIUS Act governs stablecoins" is not, yet.
3. **For SBA fees, ask which fiscal year the loan is approved in before quoting a number.**
4. **Name the vehicle** when a rule arrived somewhere unexpected — the CBDC ban riding a housing bill is exactly the kind of thing a careful reader gets wrong.
5. **Quote the as-of date** whenever you quote a figure from this file, and flag expiry.
6. **Separate the verified from the reported.** The rural-area definition above is the current example: useful, load-bearing, and not confirmed from primary text. Say which you are giving.

If you find an item here has gone stale — a search shows it moved — say so plainly, give the newer position with its source and date, and recommend the student confirm it with the official source before acting.

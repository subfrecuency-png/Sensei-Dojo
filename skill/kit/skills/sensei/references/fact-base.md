## Credit reporting law (FCRA)

- **[A] §611 is the reinvestigation duty, not §609.** The bureau must complete reinvestigation within 30 days of notice; extendable to 45 only if the consumer supplies additional relevant information during the initial 30, and not extendable at all if the item is found inaccurate or unverifiable. Written results due within 5 business days of completion. *15 U.S.C. §1681i*
- **[A] §609 is disclosure only.** It entitles a consumer to the information in their file, its sources, and who received the report. It creates **no** duty to verify, no documentary production requirement, and **no basis for deletion**. The entire "609 letter" industry rests on citing the wrong section. *15 U.S.C. §1681g*
- **[A] Route disputes through the bureau, not the furnisher.** §623(a) imposes a general accuracy duty on furnishers but carries **no private right of action**. §623(b) does — and is triggered *only* when the furnisher receives notice from a consumer reporting agency. Disputing directly with the furnisher forfeits the enforceable claim. *15 U.S.C. §1681s-2*
- **[A] Method of verification is a 15-day right.** After reinvestigation the consumer may request a description of the procedure used, including the furnisher's name and address. A vague or non-responsive answer is itself evidence the reinvestigation was unreasonable. *§611(a)(7)*
- **[A] Reinsertion requires certification plus 5-day notice.** Deleted information may not be reinserted unless the furnisher certifies it is complete and accurate; the bureau must then notify in writing within 5 business days. Failure to send that notice is a discrete, provable violation. *§611(a)(5)(B)*
- **[A] Bureaus may refuse a dispute they deem frivolous**, with notice and reasons within 5 business days. This provision was written for mass-mailed template disputes and it works. *§611(a)(3)*
- **[A] Seven years — plus 180 days for charge-offs and collections.** The period runs 7 years from the expiration of 180 days after the date of first delinquency preceding the collection or charge-off. **DOFD is the single most important date on a derogatory tradeline.** *§605(a)(4), (c)(1)*
- **[A] Bankruptcy: 10 years** from the date of entry of the order for relief — the statutory maximum for all chapters. The 7-year figure commonly cited for Chapter 13 is voluntary bureau policy, not law. *§605(a)(1)*
- **[A] Identity theft block in 4 business days** on proof of identity, an identity theft report, identification of the information, and a statement it was unauthorized. A police report is not required — an FTC Identity Theft Report suffices. *§605B*
- **[A] Willful violations:** actual damages or statutory damages of $100–$1,000, plus punitives and fee-shifting. Negligent: actual damages plus fees. Fee-shifting is why FCRA cases are viable on contingency. *§616, §617*
- **[A] Security freezes are free nationwide**, placed within one business day online or by phone. A freeze does not affect your score. *§605A(i)*
- **[B] Tax liens and civil judgments were removed entirely** from consumer reports in 2017–2018 under the National Consumer Assistance Plan.

## Business credit

- **[A] Business credit reports are largely outside the FCRA.** The dispute rights, 30-day clock, accuracy duties and private right of action that govern your personal file mostly do not exist for your business file. Errors there are a customer-service problem, not a statutory one.
- **[B] PAYDEX 80 = paying exactly on terms**, zero days beyond due. ~90 is about 20 days early; ~100 about 30 days early. Bands: 0–49 high risk, 50–79 moderate, 80–100 low risk. Paying on time *caps* you at 80.
- **[B] PAYDEX requires a D-U-N-S number and roughly three trade experiences** from two or more vendors before a score is calculated. The first objective is not a good score — it is having one.
- **[B] FICO SBSS blends business data with the owner's personal credit.** "Building business credit" never means the personal file stops mattering.
- **[A] SBA sunset the SBSS prescreen for 7(a) Small Loans.** *Procedural Notice 5000-875701.* Note: sources date this 16 Jan 2026 (SBA notice) versus 1 Mar 2026 (operational cutover) — flag the discrepancy rather than picking silently. Lender-level policy may still use the score. *(As of July 2026.)*
- **[C] Inconsistent name, address and phone is the leading cause of fragmented business files.**
- **[C] Not all vendors report**, and those that do report to different bureaus. Verify before you buy.
- **[C] Most business cards pull personal credit and require a personal guarantee.**
- **[C] Entity type does not sever personal liability on a guaranteed obligation.**
- **[F] Shelf corporations do not carry transferable creditworthiness.**
- D-U-N-S numbers, EINs and SAM.gov registration are **free**. Paying for them is the tell of a low-value program — and for SAM.gov it matches a documented GSA/BBB fraud pattern.

## Tax doctrine

- **[A] Failure to file costs ten times failure to pay** — 5%/month versus 0.5%/month. An extension extends time to file, never time to pay. Not filing because you cannot pay is the most expensive common mistake in tax administration. *IRC 6651*
- **[A] The Cohan rule does not rescue travel, meals, gifts, or listed property.** IRC 274(d) expressly withholds estimation from exactly the categories people fail to document. *IRC 274(d), 6001*
- **[A] Assessment statute:** 3 years; 6 if 25% of gross income omitted; unlimited for fraud or non-filing. Basis records must be kept until 3 years after the return reporting the sale. *IRC 6501*
- **[A] Refund claims expire** 3 years from filing or 2 years from payment, whichever is later. *IRC 6511*
- **[A] Reliance on a professional is a real defence to the 20% accuracy penalty** — unless the adviser promoted the transaction or you withheld facts. *IRC 6662, 6664(c); Neonatology Associates, 115 T.C. 43*
- **[C] S-corp reasonable compensation is a facts test and low salaries lose.** *Watson, 668 F.3d 1008 (8th Cir. 2012); Radtke, 895 F.2d 1196 (7th Cir. 1990)*
- **[B] Home office requires regular AND exclusive use.** Safe harbour $5/sq ft to 300 sq ft. *IRC 280A(c)(1)*
- **[B] Without a written accountable plan, reimbursing yourself creates taxable wages** — and IRC 67(g) means you cannot deduct it personally either. *Reg. 1.62-2*
- **§179 vs bonus:** §179 is limited to business taxable income (excess carries forward); bonus has no income limit and can create a loss — which the excess business loss threshold may then suspend.
- **SSTB cliff:** health, law, accounting, actuarial science, performing arts, consulting, athletics, financial services, brokerage, and any business whose principal asset is the reputation or skill of its owners. Above the full phase-out the deduction is **eliminated entirely**, not reduced.

## Capital access — debt

- **[A] SBA does not make grants to start or expand a for-profit business.** Federal money reaching a for-profit is almost always SBIR/STTR, sector cost-share (e.g. USDA REAP), or a state/nonprofit pass-through.
- **[A] SBA 7(a) guaranty is 85% at or below $150,000 and 75% above** — not 90%. Max loan $5,000,000; max SBA exposure $3,750,000. **SBA Express is a separate product with a 50% maximum guaranty** — the widely-quoted "50% to 85%" range conflates the two and misstates the economics.
- **[A] The pricing cliffs are arithmetic, and they are large:**
  - **$150,000** — guaranty steps 85% → 75%
  - **$350,000** — max rate spread tightens base + 4.5% → base + 3.0%. A borrower asking $340,000 shops a market 150bp worse than one asking $360,000.
  - **$700,000 / $1,000,000** — upfront fee steps 3.0% → 3.5%, then 3.75% on the guaranteed portion above $1M
- **[A] SBA sets 7(a) and 504 fees every fiscal year by notice — the approval date picks the schedule.** The FY2026 schedule (0% upfront fee on 7(a) loans ≤ $950,000 to manufacturers, NAICS 31–33) **expired 30 September 2026**. For loans approved **1 October 2026 through 30 September 2027**, SBA Information Notice 5000-881797 sets a 0% upfront fee on 7(a) loans **≤ $700,000** to manufacturers, food-supply-chain businesses and businesses located in a rural area. *Verified against the SBA notice, 1 October 2026.* Full FY2027 schedule: `sensei-live`.
- **[A] SBA microloans cap at $50,000** and are issued through non-profit intermediaries, which set their own rates — not through banks.
- **[B] SBA lending creates roughly 3 to 3.5 jobs per $1,000,000 lent.** Effects are larger for younger firms and **larger where local credit conditions are weak** — evidence the loans relieve a genuinely binding constraint rather than substituting for available private credit. *Brown & Earle, Journal of Finance 72(3), 2017 — linked administrative data on all SBA loans, lenders and US employers*
- **[A] An SBA loan is not government money.** It is a private lender's loan with a partial federal guarantee. The lender underwrites, prices, approves and collects. **The guarantee protects the lender, not you**, and personal guarantees are standard.
- **[A] Regulation B is a free diagnostic after any decline.** Over $1,000,000 in gross revenues you get written reasons *only if you request them in writing within 60 days*. Below that, notification within 30 days, possibly oral. Almost nobody asks. *12 CFR §1002.9*
- **[A] Small banks fully approved 57% of applicants** — the highest of any lender type. Online lenders drew 29% of applications, and 60% of their borrowers reported costs higher than expected (vs 37% small banks, 32% large banks). Only **42%** of applicants got the full amount sought; **22%** got nothing. Correct sequence: small bank / credit union → CDFI or SBA microlender → large bank → online lender last. *Federal Reserve, 2026 Report on Employer Firms — convenience sample, not random. Treat the ordering as reliable and the decimals as not.*
- **[A] 59% of firms carrying debt used a personal guarantee**; 51% pledged business assets.
- **[C] 1,432 Treasury-certified CDFIs** as of winter 2025; the CDFI Fund awarded 357 institutions $408.2M in FY2024 Financial Assistance. CDFIs are a **different underwriting box**, not a worse-priced version of the same one — they are mandated toward borrowers conventional underwriting declines.
- **[A] Federal thresholds moved 1 October 2025:** micro-purchase $10,000 → **$15,000**; simplified acquisition $250,000 → **$350,000**; subcontracting plan $750,000 → **$900,000**. Under FAR 19.502-2(a) that band is where small-business set-aside is the **default**.
- **[A] California usury: business purpose moves you to a higher cap, it does not remove the cap.** Points and fees count toward the ceiling under the anti-evasion clause. The exemption actually used is the real-estate-broker clause — a loan made **or arranged** by a CA-licensed broker and secured at least in part by real property. *Cal. Const. Art. XV §1*
- **[C] Ten investors is a wall, not a speed bump** for fractionalized trust deeds. *B&P §10229; Corp. Code §25102.5*
- **[C] MCAs are recharacterized as loans** where the contract lacks genuine reconciliation or the provider bears no real risk of loss. FTC obtained a $20.3M judgment against operator Jonathan Braun (Feb 2024).
- **[C] A merchant cash advance's true APR cannot be computed at signing — its own regulator said so.** California SB 1235 (2018) took four years of rulemaking, took effect December 2022, and the DFPI created an **estimated-APR safe harbour** precisely because disclosed and effective APR "may vary substantially" when repayment depends on future sales. Do the arithmetic yourself: (total repayment − advance) ÷ advance, annualised over your realistic repayment period.

## Capital access — equity, grants and the securities container

- **[A] An early-stage SBIR award roughly DOUBLES the probability of subsequently raising venture capital**, with large positive effects on patenting and revenue, and **the largest effects among the most financially constrained firms**. The mechanism is **funding prototyping — not certification or signalling**, so the benefit accrues even if no investor ever learns of the award. *Howell, American Economic Review 107(4), 2017 — regression discontinuity on ranked DOE SBIR applicants, 7,436 firms, $884M in awards.* **This is the highest expected-value capital available to a technical founder, and it costs zero equity.** Eleven federal agencies run SBIR/STTR on differing solicitation cycles.
- **[A] Angel funding causally improves the business — survival, employment, patenting, web traffic, exits.** *Kerr, Lerner & Schoar, Review of Financial Studies 27(1), 2014 — regression discontinuity on angel-group interest thresholds.*
- **[A] But angel funding showed NO effect on access to further financing at the discontinuity.** The value landed in the operations, not in the signal to the next investor. **If the thesis for the round is "it signals Series A," the best evidence available does not support it.**
- **[A] Accelerator cash and co-working space alone showed no measurable effect on venture performance. Only the bundled schooling did.** *Gonzalez-Uribe & Leatherbee, Review of Financial Studies 31(4), 2018 — regression discontinuity on Start-Up Chile.* Evaluate a programme on curriculum. Boundary: studied an ecosystem accelerator taking young early-stage ventures; generalisation to top-tier selective programmes is **not established and cannot be**, because their selectivity is what removes the design.
- **[B] Crowdfunding validates that a crowd will pay — nothing else.** Over **75% of funded Kickstarter projects deliver late, and more funding predicts more delay** (*Mollick, Journal of Business Venturing 29(1), 2014 — 48,500+ projects*). Compared against an expert panel, crowds and experts largely agree; where they diverge, **the crowd funds projects experts would not** — more permissive, not better (*Mollick & Nanda, Management Science 62(6), 2016*). Overfunding is a scaling problem arriving before the capability to absorb it.
- **[C] Exemption caps:** Reg CF **$5,000,000** per 12 months. Reg A Tier 1 **$20,000,000**; Tier 2 **$75,000,000**, with ongoing reporting. Rule 506(b) and 506(c) are **unlimited** in amount. Form D is filed after first sale. Verify current text at sec.gov — caps have been amended more than once.
- **[A] The trap that catches founders: Rule 506(b) prohibits general solicitation.** A public post, open newsletter, livestreamed pitch or podcast describing the offering can constitute general solicitation and **destroy the exemption**, potentially giving investors rescission rights. 506(b) permits unlimited accredited plus **up to 35 non-accredited sophisticated** purchasers.
- **[A] To raise publicly you must be in Rule 506(c) — and then every purchaser must be accredited AND verified by the issuer.** Self-certification is not sufficient under 506(c). *17 CFR 230.506*
- **[C] Accredited investor (17 CFR 230.501):** net worth over **$1,000,000** excluding primary residence, **or** income over **$200,000** individually / **$300,000** jointly in each of the two most recent years with reasonable expectation of the same; **or**, since the 2020 amendments, certain professional certifications and knowledgeable employees of the fund. **Nominal thresholds are unchanged as of 2026** — the income test dates to 1982 and the net-worth test to the 2011 primary-residence exclusion, so in real terms they now reach far more households than when written. Dodd-Frank requires SEC review every four years; reviews were completed 2015, 2019, 2023.
- **[D] Venture return concentration is real in direction and unverifiable in magnitude.** Every precise figure — "5% of investments generate 50%+ of returns" — comes from proprietary datasets with undisclosed survivorship. **The part that matters to a founder survives regardless: a fund needing one investment to return the whole fund is structurally required to push every portfolio company toward an outlier outcome.** That incentive may not match a business that would be excellent at moderate scale. Understand it before taking the money, not after.
- **[D] Demographic funding shares** (≈1% of US venture to Black-founded companies, ≈1.9% to all-women teams) come from commercial deal databases with incomplete private-deal coverage and partly inferred tagging. **Direction robust across sources; the decimals are not.** The stronger evidence concerns mechanism: the gap narrows substantially when a Black partner leads the investment team, and such partners rely **less** on observable credentialing proxies — consistent with screening on proxies rather than on venture quality.
- **The routing that follows from the above:** SBIR allocates by **technical review of a written proposal** (tier A evidence of effect). SBA-guaranteed lending allocates by **financial underwriting** (tier B). CDFI lending is **mandated toward underserved borrowers** (tier C documentation). For any founder disadvantaged by network-based screening, these are not consolation prizes — **two of the three carry stronger causal evidence than venture capital does.**

## Digital assets — the legal container

- **[A] The GENIUS Act is law.** *Public Law 119-27, enacted 18 July 2025, 139 Stat. 419* — the first federal framework for payment stablecoins: permitted issuers, reserve requirements, redemption rights, supervision.
- **[A] Its effective date is the EARLIER of 18 January 2027 (18 months after enactment) or 120 days after the primary federal payment stablecoin regulators issue final implementing regulations.** *Section 20, P.L. 119-27 — verified against the enrolled text on govinfo.gov, 1 October 2026.* Late rules cannot push the date out; 18 January 2027 is a ceiling. As of 1 October 2026 the regulators were still at the proposed-rule stage (Treasury NPRM published 18 August 2026, comments due 19 October 2026; Federal Reserve proposals 24 September 2026), so a final rule issued now would trigger a date after 18 January 2027 — **18 January 2027 governs.** *(An earlier version of this file said "later of". That was wrong and inverted the planning advice.)*
- **[A] Market structure is not settled.** The CLARITY Act (H.R. 3633) **is not law**. It passed the House in July 2025; a Senate Banking substitute advanced 15–9 on 14 May 2026; on **15 September 2026 the Senate rejected cloture on the motion to proceed, 49–50**, which practitioners read as ending its prospects for this Congress. *(As of 1 October 2026.)* Anyone describing SEC/CFTC jurisdiction as resolved by statute is describing a failed bill.
- **[A] The DeFi front-end broker rule was repealed** by CRA joint resolution *H.J.Res. 25*, signed 10 April 2025 — and the CRA bars a substantially similar rule without new legislation. **Custodial broker reporting was not repealed.**
- **[A] A federal CBDC is prohibited through 31 December 2030.** Title XI of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (**P.L. 119-101**, became law without signature on 11 July 2026) bars the Federal Reserve from issuing one, directly or through an intermediary, carving out open, permissionless, private dollar-denominated currency. Graded A on enactment — **read the enrolled text before relying on the exact carve-out wording or the title's effective date.** *(As of 1 October 2026.)*
- **[A] EO 14233 (6 March 2025) established a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve** from forfeited assets and directs that it not be sold. **[D] The commonly quoted holdings figure of roughly 328,372 BTC is an analytics estimate, not a Treasury balance-sheet disclosure.**
- **[B] Three states enacted "bitcoin reserve" laws in 2025 by three materially different mechanisms.** New Hampshire HB 302 *authorises* up to 5% of public funds into assets above $500B market cap. Arizona HB 2749 uses *unclaimed and seized* property. Texas SB 21 + HB 4488 created a reserve whose first purchase was **$5M of a spot ETF, not self-custodied coin**. All three were reported in identical language. **An authorisation is not a mandate, and buying an ETF is not holding the asset. Read the instrument** — the same discipline that separates a lease from a licence.
- **[A] SEC generic listing standards (approved 17 September 2025)** let qualifying spot commodity and crypto ETPs list without individual Section 19(b) filings, compressing timelines toward roughly 60–75 days. Actively managed, leveraged and novel-feature products still require 19b-4.
- **[C] The SEC's crypto positions are interpretation and proposal, not statute.** On 23 March 2026 the SEC published a Commission **interpretation** (with CFTC guidance) on how the securities laws apply to certain types of crypto assets (Federal Register doc. 2026-05635). On 18 August 2026 it **proposed** "Regulation Crypto Assets", including tiered fundraising exemptions; comments were due 20 October 2026. *(As of 1 October 2026.)* An interpretation states the agency's view and can be revised by a later Commission; a proposal binds nothing. Neither is law passed by Congress — and the CLARITY Act, which would have supplied a statutory framework, failed cloture on 15 September 2026.
- **[A] Nothing in this container carries deposit insurance.** FDIC insurance covers deposits at insured banks. SIPC does not reach crypto unless it is a security in a securities account. A stablecoin's backing is a private balance-sheet obligation — the GENIUS Act imposes reserve and redemption duties on issuers, **it does not make the government the guarantor.** The same asymmetry Sensei teaches everywhere else: *regulated means supervised, not safe.*

## Digital assets — tax, reporting and the balance sheet

- **[A] Digital assets are property, not securities, for federal tax.** Every disposition is a taxable event — including crypto-to-crypto swaps and spending it. **No like-kind treatment. §1031 was narrowed to real property in 2017 and does not reach digital assets** — a point the student who knows the real-property rules will assume wrongly.
- **[A] Because they are property, §1091 wash-sale does not apply.** Selling at a loss and repurchasing immediately is currently permitted. Repeal proposals have recurred annually since 2021 and **none has been enacted**; two vehicles were live in mid-2026, one drafted to reach tax years beginning after 2025. **Do not build a permanent plan on a loophole with an annual repeal attempt and a retroactive-effective-date draft in circulation.**
- **[A] Form 1099-DA: gross proceeds from 1 January 2025, cost basis from 1 January 2026** for covered assets acquired and held in the same broker account. Your records and broker-reported basis will diverge wherever assets moved between venues. Reconcile before filing, not after a notice.
- **[B] Global tax visibility arrives on a published schedule.** OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework: 48 countries collecting from 1 January 2026, first automatic exchanges **2027**, a second wave — Switzerland, Singapore, UAE, Hong Kong, Türkiye — in **2028**, and the **United States committed to first exchanges in 2029**. The EU implements through DAC8, applying from 1 January 2026 with first reports due between 1 January and 30 September 2027. Retail payment transactions above **USD 50,000** carry specific reporting rules.
- **[A] FASB ASU 2023-08 changed the balance sheet.** For fiscal years beginning after 15 December 2024, in-scope crypto is measured at **fair value with changes through earnings** — replacing the indefinite-lived-intangible model under which impairments were recognised but gains never were until sale. **Two consequences: pre-2025 and post-2025 financials are not comparable, and holdings now inject full two-way volatility into reported earnings.** A lender looking back three years is reading two different accounting regimes.
- **The second-order trap.** Sensei's standing rule — tax minimisation and borrowing capacity pull in opposite directions — has a new edge here. Crypto under fair value makes earnings swing both ways, and **volatile earnings are volatile earnings to an underwriter.** A treasury position taken for one reader is read by every reader, including the one pricing your next DSCR loan.

## Markets — what the return record actually shows

Where the student asks what to do with retained earnings, or brings you a manager, a fund, or a strategy.

- **[B] Active management underperforms, and the failure rate rises with horizon.** Over the 20 years to end-2024, **94.1%** of US domestic funds underperformed the S&P Composite 1500 — **97.3%** on a risk-adjusted basis. Of 18 domestic categories only two had fewer than 90% underperforming; large value was "best" at 87.8%. *S&P Dow Jones Indices SPIVA — published methodology, near-census of the fund universe.* Graded B, not A: the methodology has published critiques on benchmark selection and share-class treatment. **The direction is robust across methods; the decimals are not.**
- **[B] Cost is the most reliable predictor of future fund returns tested — better than past performance.** Cheapest quintile against priciest: US equity 62% vs 20% success, international equity 51% vs 21%, taxable bond 59% vs 17%. *Morningstar fee studies.* Note the admission against interest — the finding says fees beat the publisher's own star ratings.
- **[A] The arithmetic, so the student can check it.** $100,000 at 7% gross over 30 years: at 0.03% it becomes $754,849; at 1.00%, $574,349; at 2.00%, **$432,194**. **A 2% annual fee consumes 43% of terminal wealth**, and none of it is contingent on performance.
- **[B] Frequent trading destroys returns — replicated across three markets and three decades.** US: the most active quintile of 66,465 households returned 11.4% against a 17.9% market, a **6.5-point annual drag** (*Barber & Odean, 2000*). Taiwan: across **3.7 billion transactions**, day traders lost **23.9bp per day net of fees**, aggregate performance was reliably negative in **14 of 15 years**, and consistently profitable day traders were about **5%** of active traders. Brazil equity futures: of those persisting beyond 300 days, **97% lost money** and 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage. **One direction, three independent administrative datasets.**
- **[A] Most published strategy research does not replicate.** Of **447** tested anomalies, **64% were insignificant at the 5% level**; applying the t > 3.0 threshold proposed for a literature that had already published **316 factors**, **85% were insignificant**. *Hou, Xue & Zhang, "Replicating Anomalies"; Harvey, Liu & Zhu, RFS 2016.* Report the dispute honestly — *Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen, Journal of Finance 2023* reach a more optimistic conclusion on a Bayesian hierarchical framework. **What is not disputed is that searching thousands of specifications manufactures false positives at scale.** A backtest with no out-of-sample period, no specification count and no transaction costs is tier D whatever its Sharpe ratio.
- **[C] A long-run compound return can be almost entirely one year. Check before treating it as a process.** Calendar-year returns 2015 through July 2026 — commercial series with disclosed methodology, cross-checked against a second series; underlying exchange prices are not independently auditable. The arithmetic below is computed, not quoted:

| | Bitcoin | Ethereum | S&P 500 total return |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $10,000 becomes | $2,070,803 | $59,989 | $44,013 |
| **Compound annual rate** | **55.96%** | **19.62%** | **13.14%** |
| Arithmetic mean year | 174.19% | 94.21% | 14.12% |
| **Median year** | **75.94%** | **21.62%** | **18.14%** |
| Up years / down years | 8 / 4 | **5 / 5** | 10 / 2 |
| **$10,000 removing the best year** | **$140,976** | **$10,538** | $33,473 |
| **Share of result from one year** | **93.2%** | **82.4%** | 23.9% |

  **93.2% of Bitcoin's entire eleven-and-a-half-year result came from 2017 alone.** For Ethereum, 82.4% came from 2020 — strip that year and a decade of holding returned **5.4% in total**, on an asset up in five years and down in five. **Neither headline compound rate describes a repeatable process.** Note also how far the arithmetic mean sits above both the median and the compound rate. For volatile assets the mean is the number no holder ever received, and it is the one most often quoted at you.
- **[A] The σ²/2 volatility-drag shortcut fails at high volatility — Sensei had this wrong before testing it.** The approximation estimates the gap between arithmetic mean and compound return well for equities: 1.02 points predicted against 0.98 observed for the S&P 500. For Bitcoin it predicts roughly **700 points against 118 observed** — wrong by a factor of six. **The drag is real and enormous; the textbook shortcut for estimating it is not valid at that scale.** Use observed arithmetic minus geometric. *A master who will not correct himself in front of the student teaches the wrong lesson twice.*
- **[A] Drawdown recovery is non-linear, and this is pure arithmetic:** −20% needs **+25%** · −50% needs **+100%** · −77% needs **+335%** · −84% needs **+525%** · −93% needs **+1,329%**. The same curve governs a leveraged property that loses equity.
- **[C] Correlation estimated from a decade of annual returns is worthless, and is quoted constantly.** Computed 2015–2026: Bitcoin against the S&P 500 gives 0.318; Bitcoin against Ethereum gives 0.271. **Do not use either.** With ten to twelve observations the standard error swamps the estimate — and a 0.27 correlation between Bitcoin and Ethereum is self-evidently wrong as a description of two assets that move together. The annual frequency destroyed the information. **This is "who is missing from the sample" wearing a different hat: what is missing is the observations.**
- **[C] A cyclical pattern with n = 3 is a pattern, not a distribution.** Bitcoin's halving-to-peak interval has run **17, 18 and 18 months**; peak-to-trough has run **12 and 12**. Tight — and built on three observations and two respectively, with no established causal mechanism, promoted overwhelmingly by holders, and **already diverging**: at month nine past the peak, prior cycles were down roughly 65% and 70% while the current one is down 47%. The market also changed structurally in between — spot ETFs from January 2024, generic listing standards from September 2025, fair-value accounting from FY2025, federal stablecoin law from July 2025, tax reporting from January 2026. **A model fitted before those breaks is out of sample now.** And the next halving date is itself an estimate: halvings occur at a block height, not a calendar date, and published 2028 estimates span 26 March to 7 May.
- **[B] Reported crypto exchange volume is contaminated.** Wash trading averaged **over 70%** of reported volume on unregulated exchanges — roughly 77.5% on average, over 81.7% on unregulated Tier-2 venues, and up to 53.4% on some Tier-1 venues; an estimated $4.5tn of spot and $1.5tn of derivatives volume in Q1 2020 alone. *Cong, Li, Tang & Yang, "Crypto Wash Trading," NBER w30783; Management Science, 2023.* **Every metric derived from reported volume inherits this — liquidity rankings, "most traded" lists, volume-weighted indices. On-chain settlement data is materially more trustworthy because it is externally verifiable.**
- **[D] Roughly 53% of the ~20.2 million tokens listed between mid-2021 and end-2025 are no longer actively traded**, with 11.6 million dying in 2025 alone. *CoinGecko / GeckoTerminal — commercial research, but with disclosed methodology, which is more than most.* Dominated by launchpad memecoins, so it says little about assets with meaningful capitalisation. **What it does establish: the modal token is worthless, and any strategy sampling from "new tokens" draws from that distribution.**

## Counterparty verification — what "audited" and "regulated" actually mean

The highest-yield question Sensei can teach. It applies to a custodian, a lender, a processor, a fund, a sponsor, a property manager and a vendor alike.

- **[C] "Audited" is four different things, and vendors use them interchangeably.** Ranked by assurance:
  1. **Financial statement audit** (PCAOB/ISA) — an opinion that statements are fairly presented in all material respects. Covers assets **and liabilities**. The auditor carries legal liability.
  2. **Examination / attestation** (ISAE 3000) — an opinion on a specific subject matter against stated criteria. Real assurance, narrower scope. Read the criteria.
  3. **Agreed-upon procedures** — the practitioner performs steps *the client selected* and reports findings. **No opinion. No assurance.** Nearly worthless alone.
  4. **Self-attestation** — an assertion wearing a noun.
  **Four questions settle it: audited by whom, under what standard, of what subject matter, and may I read the opinion?** A missing answer to any one of the four *is* the answer. The same four questions apply to a syndicator's "audited" operating statements.
- **[B] "Proof of reserves" is not proof of solvency.** The Merkle-tree construction genuinely proves that stated addresses held stated amounts at a moment, and that your balance sat in the hashed liability set. It does **not** prove liabilities; does not prevent assets being **borrowed for the snapshot and returned after**; **cannot detect accounts omitted from the tree** — your inclusion proof says nothing about who else was left out; and does not prove exclusive key control. **Solvency is reserves minus liabilities. A reserves-only attestation shows one term.** Proof of solvency requires proof of liabilities alongside it, and zero-knowledge constructions for it exist. Verify which one a counterparty actually publishes rather than which one they name.
- **[A] "Licensed" and "registered" mean an entity met entry conditions and is subject to examination.** They do not mean solvent, honest, or backstopped. **Verify the claim itself, free, in minutes:** NMLS Consumer Access (money transmission and mortgage origination), FINRA BrokerCheck and SEC IAPD (securities), FDIC BankFind (bank status), NCUA (credit unions), SEC EDGAR (sworn filings), the state secretary of state (entity standing), and the county recorder (who actually owns the property and what is against it). **A firm claiming regulation that appears in none of these has told you something.**
- **[C] A blockchain proves transactions, not identities.** The ledger is high-grade evidence of what transfers occurred. Who controlled the keys is an **inference layer** built on clustering heuristics and off-chain attribution. In *United States v. Sterlingov* one vendor's methodology survived a Daubert challenge and was admitted — a real result, but provider-specific, with clustering parameters held as trade secrets and the court expressly preserving factual challenge before the jury. **Admissible is not the same as accurate.**

## Base rates — the numbers that reframe the rest

- **[C] Roughly half of new US employer establishments survive five years.** BLS Business Employment Dynamics, a near-census of administrative unemployment-insurance records: **77–84%** survive 1 year, **55–63%** survive 3, **42–50%** survive 5. Recession-birth cohorts fare worst; those surviving four years do materially better thereafter. **"90% of startups fail" is not true of businesses generally and has no primary source.**
- **[C] Most businesses are funded by their founders.** Of US employer businesses: **personal and family savings 63.9%**, bank business loans **17.9%**, personal credit cards **10.3%**. About 90% need some startup capital and roughly two-thirds get it from savings. *Kauffman Foundation calculations from Census Annual Survey of Entrepreneurs.* **Venture capital, angels and accelerators describe an extreme minority path** — most published "startup funding" advice is written about someone else.
- **The concentration test belongs here too.** See *Markets* above: 93.2% of one widely-cited eleven-year compound return came from a single calendar year. **Whenever a rate is offered as evidence of a process, ask what survives deleting the best period.** The same question exposes a customer list carried by one account, a channel carried by one campaign, and a portfolio carried by one property.

## Real property — the tax engine

Real estate is not a high-return asset class unlevered and pre-tax. It is a **tax-advantaged** one, and nearly all of the advantage sits in a handful of code sections. Everything below is federal, tax year 2026, post-OBBBA (P.L. 119-21, enacted 4 July 2025).

- **[A] 100% bonus depreciation is permanent again.** OBBBA restored it for property acquired after 19 Jan 2025 and placed in service on or after 20 Jan 2025, and repealed the TCJA phase-down. Qualifying property is generally MACRS recovery period **20 years or less** — which is exactly what a cost segregation study produces. **Any advice still using phase-out urgency predates July 2025.** *IRC 168(k)*
- **[A] MACRS lives:** 27.5 residential rental · 39 nonresidential · 15 land improvements and qualified improvement property · 5 and 7 personal property · **land never**. Land allocation therefore controls the deduction; an appraisal split is more defensible than the assessor's ratio, which is common practice and **not** a safe harbour. *IRC 168*
- **[A] New IRC 168(n)** allows elective 100% expensing of the *building shell* for nonresidential real property used in US manufacturing, production or refining. First time in modern law the structure itself is expensable. Narrow, and transformative for an industrial owner-user.
- **[A] Section 179 reaches roofs, HVAC, fire protection and security on nonresidential property placed in service after the building.** Bonus does not. But 179 cannot create a loss and bonus can. **Order it: 179 first for what bonus cannot reach, bonus for the rest.** 179 is generally unavailable for residential rental. *IRC 179(d), (f)*
- **[C] Cost segregation typically reclassifies 15–35% of improvement basis** into 20-year-or-less classes; 15–25% is typical for plain apartment or office. No government dataset publishes this. **A percentage promised before the study is a sales number, not a finding.**
- **[A] A look-back study needs no amended returns** — cumulative catch-up as a 481(a) adjustment on Form 3115, automatic method change. The most under-used move available to anyone who has held property 3–10 years. *Rev. Proc. 2015-13*
- **[A] Then the bill arrives. Section 1245 recapture is ordinary income with no cap** — everything a cost seg moved to 5- and 7-year life comes back at up to 37% plus state plus NIIT. Straight-line depreciation on the shell returns as **unrecaptured 1250 gain capped at 25%**. Fifteen-year land improvements are 1250, not 1245; **many popular sources say otherwise and are wrong.** *IRC 1245, 1250*
- **[A] Recapture is not deferrable by installment sale.** 453(i) requires it recognised in full in the year of disposition even if you receive no cash. On a seller-financed exit of cost-segregated property the tax can exceed the down payment. Reg. 1.453-12 also recognises unrecaptured 1250 gain **first** as payments arrive. *IRC 453(i)*
- **[A] Section 453A charges interest on the deferral** where sale price exceeds $150,000 and aggregate outstanding installment obligations at year end exceed $5,000,000.
- **[A] Section 1031 is intact and unchanged after OBBBA.** The $500,000 annual cap proposal did not pass. Real property only since TCJA. **45 days to identify in writing to the qualified intermediary; 180 days to close OR the return due date including extensions, whichever is earlier** — a Q4 relinquishment silently shortens to April unless you extend, so extend. Boot: **you must replace both the equity and the debt.** *IRC 1031*
- **[A] Reverse and improvement exchanges have a safe harbour** — an exchange accommodation titleholder parks title, same 45/180 clocks from parking. *Rev. Proc. 2000-37*
- **[A] A Delaware Statutory Trust interest is like-kind real property** *(Rev. Rul. 2004-86)* — the standard answer when 45 days will not produce a replacement. Price the constraint honestly: the seven prohibitions forbid new capital, refinancing, re-leasing except on tenant insolvency, reinvestment and more than minor improvements, and require quarterly distribution. **A DST is legally unable to adapt.** A partnership interest is **not** like-kind — syndication LP units cannot be exchanged.
- **[A] Section 121 excludes $250,000 / $500,000, and has not been indexed since 1997.** Own and occupy as principal residence 2 of the last 5 years. **The exception that makes the strategy work:** periods *after* the last date it was your principal residence are not nonqualified use — live in it two years, move out, rent up to three more, sell inside the window, keep the full exclusion. **The reverse order does not work.** Depreciation *allowed or allowable* after 6 May 1997 is never excluded. *IRC 121(b)(5), (d)(6)*
- **[A] Section 469 decides whether your paper losses are worth anything, and most people clear none of the three doors.**
  - **The $25,000 allowance** — active participation, phased out $100,000–$150,000 MAGI. Written in 1986, never indexed. Effectively closed to anyone earning enough to want it. *469(i)*
  - **Real estate professional status** — more than 50% of *all* personal services **and** more than 750 hours in real property trades or businesses, plus material participation per activity unless you file the 1.469-9(g) aggregation election. **A real estate licence does not confer it.** A full-time W-2 job makes the 50% test arithmetically brutal. The failure mode is documentation; courts reject reconstructed logs. *469(c)(7)*
  - **The short-term rental exception** — average period of customer use **7 days or less** means the activity is **not a rental activity at all**, so the per-se passive rule never applies and ordinary material participation decides. **REPS is not required.** Average is total rental days divided by number of separate stays, recomputed every December because it creeps. *Reg. 1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii)(A)*
- **[A] Self-rental is asymmetric by design:** net income from property leased to a business you materially participate in is recharacterised **nonpassive**, while net losses stay **passive** and suspend. The fix is the **1.469-4 grouping election** — in writing, binding absent a material change in facts. *Reg. 1.469-2(f)(6)*
- **[A] Hotel-like services cost you the rental exclusion from self-employment tax.** Daily cleaning during a stay, linens, meals, concierge, transport push you to Schedule C and 15.3%. The tension is real, because those same services help the 30-day exception. *IRC 1402(a)(1); Reg. 1.1402(a)-4(c)(2)*
- **[A] Section 199A is permanent under OBBBA and rental real estate can qualify.** Safe harbour: separate books and records, **250 hours** of rental services (a property manager's hours count), contemporaneous records. **Triple-net-leased property and taxpayer-residence property are excluded from the safe harbour** — NNN owners must argue 162 trade-or-business status on facts. *Rev. Proc. 2019-38*
- **[A] The 163(j)(7)(B) election-out is now an expensive trade.** Electing out of the interest limitation puts you on ADS — 40 / 30 / 20 years, straight line — and **ADS property is ineligible for bonus depreciation.** Before OBBBA this cost little; with 100% bonus permanent it is a large surrender. **[A] The IRS has opened a retroactive withdrawal of elections made in tax years 2022, 2023 and 2024** *(as of July 2026)*. For anyone affected this is the highest-dollar item on the board. Confirm the current procedure with a CPA before the window closes.
- **[A] Section 461(l) is the second gate, after 469.** $256,000 / $512,000 for 2026 — *lower* than 2025 because OBBBA reset the base to $250,000 / $500,000 indexed from 2024, and made the limitation permanent. Excess becomes an NOL carryforward, not a lost deduction, and NOLs are then capped at 80% of taxable income. **A plan to erase a very high W-2 in one year is arithmetic fiction.**
- **[A] Opportunity Zones: two programmes are running at once, and conflating them is expensive.**
  - **OZ 1.0 deferred gains become taxable in the tax year including 31 December 2026** — on the 2026 return filed April 2027 — whether or not the fund has been sold and whether or not it produced a dollar of cash. Taxed on the **lesser of** the original deferred gain **or** the fund's fair market value on 31 Dec 2026, which makes a defensible year-end valuation worth real money. **The 10-year step-up on fund-level appreciation survives** — do not sell a fund near its ten-year mark to pay this tax. Plan the liquidity separately. *IRC 1400Z-2*
  - **OZ 2.0 is permanent, effective 1 January 2027** through 31 Dec 2033, with governors nominating by late September 2026 (extension to 28 Oct 2026) *(as of July 2026)*. At least **25% of each state's zones must be rural**. **Rural funds receive a 30% basis step-up instead of 10%, and the substantial-improvement test is halved from 100% of basis to 50%.** The halved test is worth more than the step-up in most pro formas — it changes which deals pencil at all.
- **[A] Section 1014 is the actual exit strategy.** Heirs take fair market value at death; all deferred gain **and all recapture** disappear. Combine it with 1031 and the highest-value real estate tax strategy in the code is not clever — exchange indefinitely, never sell, die owning it. Nothing beats a 100% exclusion.
- **[A] The exit stack for a high earner in a high-tax state:** 1245 recapture at ordinary + 3.8% NIIT · unrecaptured 1250 at 25% + 3.8% · appreciation at 20% + 3.8% · state 0–13.3%. **Character, not rate, is the part you control.**
- **[A] Land carries its own rules.**
  - **Section 266** lets you elect *annually* to capitalise property tax, mortgage interest, insurance and other carrying charges into basis on unimproved, unproductive land. Raw land throws off no income, so those deductions are frequently worthless — suspended, SALT-limited, or exceeding available income. This converts a dead deduction into basis. Year by year, category by category. Almost nobody uses it. *Reg. 1.266-1*
  - **Dealer versus investor is decided per property, not per taxpayer.** Dealer property is inventory: ordinary income, self-employment tax, and **no 1031 and no 453**. Frequency of sales and improvement activity carry the most weight. Segregate flip inventory from hold inventory into separate entities with separate documented intent from the first day, not the first audit.
  - **Section 1237** gives a non-dealer capital gain on the **first five lots** from a tract held five years with **no substantial improvements** (more than a 10% lift to a lot's value). From lot six, 5% of each lot's price is ordinary. **The bind: it forbids exactly the roads, water and sewer that make subdividing worth doing.**
  - Land is **not depreciable** — no shelter during the hold — but 1031 works fully, and land is the ideal 453 installment asset precisely because there is no recapture to accelerate.
- **[A] FIRPTA makes the buyer the withholding agent and personally liable.** 0% at $300,000 or less with buyer-residence use; 10% from $300,001 to $1,000,000 on the same test; **15% otherwise — on the gross price, not the gain.** Take a seller non-foreign affidavit at every closing as routine. *IRC 1445*
- **[A] Two energy incentives closed mid-2026:** 179D terminated for projects **beginning construction after 30 June 2026** (up to roughly $5.81 per square foot); 45L terminated for homes **acquired after 30 June 2026** (up to $5,000 per unit). Work that began or closed in time remains eligible — documenting *begin construction* is now the entire question.
- **[A] The 47 rehabilitation credit is 20% of qualified rehabilitation expenditures on a certified historic structure, claimed ratably over five years** at 4% a year from placed-in-service. TCJA repealed the separate 10% pre-1936 credit. Enlargement and new construction do not qualify; five-year recapture applies. **It stacks with LIHTC and Opportunity Zones** — the most reliable large subsidy still fully available for adaptive reuse.
- **[A] LIHTC got materially larger in 2026:** the 9% per-capita multiplier rose $3.00 → **$3.36** (+12%), and the private-activity-bond financing test dropped from **50% to 25%** of aggregate basis for 4% credits. The bond test is the structurally larger change — it roughly halves volume-cap consumption per deal in states that exhaust their allocation annually. *IRC 42*
- **[A] Conservation easements: 170(h)(7) disallows the deduction entirely** where a partnership's or S corporation's contribution exceeds **2.5 times partners' relevant basis**, with narrow exceptions for a three-year holding period, family-owned pass-throughs and certified historic structures. Syndicated versions are **listed transactions**. *T.D. 10007; Notice 2017-10*

## Real property — financing

- **[B] The 2026 baseline.** 30-year fixed averaged **6.58%** (Freddie Mac PMMS, week ended 23 July 2026 — highest since August 2025). Conforming one-unit **$832,750** baseline, **$1,249,125** high-cost ceiling, which is also the baseline in Alaska, Hawaii, Guam and the USVI. FHA one-unit **$541,287** floor to **$1,249,125**; two-unit $693,050–$1,599,375; three-unit $837,700–$1,933,200; four-unit $1,041,125–$2,402,625. HUD raised FHA limits 3.26%, effective for case numbers assigned on or after 1 Jan 2026.
- **[C] Cap rates, 2026:** multifamily all classes around 5.6%, Class A primary 4.50–5.25% · industrial 5.0–6.25% · retail large centres about 6.55%, strip about 6.44%, single-tenant net lease about 6.80% · office Class A 6.0–8.0%, **Class B repriced to 8.5–11%**. Use these to sanity-check a broker's assumption, never as your own.
- **[A] The arithmetic that defines this market: stabilised multifamily bought at market cap is negatively levered.** A 5.0% cap financed at 6.5% loses money on every borrowed dollar in year one. **That is not an argument against buying — it is a requirement to name where the return comes from:** rent growth, value-add NOI, assumable below-market debt, tax character, or buying below replacement cost. **If the model needs the exit cap at or below the entry cap, it is a bet, not a plan.**
- **[B] FHA house hacking is the cheapest leverage a private individual can obtain anywhere in American finance** — 3.5% down at 580+ FICO on **one to four units**, occupying one. **But the self-sufficiency test binds on three- and four-unit properties:** 75% of the appraiser's determined market rent must equal or exceed full PITI including HOA. That is a property-level test entirely separate from your DTI, and in expensive markets it is what kills the deal. **Two-unit properties are exempt**, which is why duplexes are the practical vehicle there. *HUD Handbook 4000.1*
- **[B] Cash-out refinance seasoning is 6 months on title, and any mortgage being paid off is generally 12 months old.** The **delayed financing exception** waives seasoning entirely for an all-cash purchase, capped at purchase price plus closing costs — that is what lets a cash-purchase BRRRR move at speed. Rate-and-term refinances have no seasoning requirement. *Fannie Mae Selling Guide*
- **[C] The BRRRR constraint is arithmetic, not strategy:** to recover 100% of capital at a 75% LTV refinance you must be all-in — purchase, rehab, carry and closing — **at or below 75% of after-repair value**. In 2026 most executions leave 15–30% in the deal. That is still an excellent outcome against a conventional purchase. **A model showing infinite return on zero remaining capital is not an outcome, it is an error.**
- **[B] HUD multifamily has no private-market equivalent.** 221(d)(4) is **40-year fixed**, fully amortising, non-recourse, plus up to three years interest-only construction, 87% LTV market-rate and 90% affordable. 223(f) is **35-year fixed**, 85% LTV, 87% with project-based Section 8 or LIHTC, 80% cash-out. The price is process: nine to eighteen months, Davis-Bacon wages on construction, heavy documentation. For a patient developer that is a structural advantage over merchant builders on three-year bridge debt.
- **[B] SBA 504 buys your own building with 10% down** — roughly 50% bank first lien, 40% SBA debenture, 10% equity, long-term fixed below market. Occupy at least **51%** of an existing building, or **60%** of new construction reaching **80% within ten years**. As of 1 March 2026 every direct and indirect owner must be a US citizen or national residing in the US. **The cheapest real estate capital in the country for a business that occupies its own space, and the most under-used** — owners lease for twenty years and accumulate nothing. Misrepresenting occupancy to obtain it is loan fraud.
- **[C] DSCR lending broke the constraint that used to stop investors at four to ten properties**, because qualification runs on property income rather than personal income. Floor 1.00–1.25 (1.25 typical, 1.30+ for best pricing), FICO 620–660, 20–25% down typical. The trade is 50–150 basis points and a prepayment penalty.
- **[C] Assumable below-market debt is the most under-quantified asset in this market.** Compute the present value of the interest saved over the remaining term and treat it as a discount to basis. It also makes *your* property worth more at exit than an identical one requiring payoff.
- **[A] Garn-St Germain protects a transfer into an inter vivos trust where the borrower remains a beneficiary and occupancy rights do not transfer, and a leasehold of three years or less with no purchase option. It does not protect a transfer into an LLC, and it does not protect subject-to.** *12 U.S.C. 1701j-3.* **The risk profile changed with rates:** lenders rarely called performing loans for a decade because there was nothing to gain. Calling a 3% loan is now economically rational. Anyone running subject-to without a committed payoff is short an unpriced call option written against themselves.
- **[A] Dodd-Frank's loan-originator regime reaches only dwellings the borrower will occupy.** Seller financing of commercial property, raw land and non-owner-occupied investment property sits entirely outside it — which is why it is routine there and legally fraught in owner-occupied residential. The **three-property exclusion** requires: three or fewer properties in twelve months, seller owned them, seller did not construct in the ordinary course of business, fixed rate or a reset no earlier than five years with reasonable caps, and a good-faith determination of ability to repay. *12 CFR 1026.36(a)(5)*

## Real property — law, regulation and the tenant relationship

- **[A] Seven federal protected classes — race, colour, religion, national origin, sex, disability, familial status — and your intent is irrelevant.** Liability attaches to the effect of a policy, not your state of mind, so a facially neutral rule can still be unlawful. HUD reads "sex" to include sexual orientation and gender identity after *Bostock*. **Describe the property, never the tenant you want.** *42 U.S.C. 3601 et seq.*
- **[A] Assistance animals are not pets** — no pet fee, no pet rent, no breed or weight limit, not covered by a no-pets policy. Reasonable-accommodation duties are affirmative, not passive. This one issue generates a large share of the complaints filed against small landlords.
- **[A] Source of income is not a federal protected class, but it is protected by statute in California, New York, Washington, Colorado, Virginia, Utah, New Jersey, Massachusetts and dozens of localities** — the list is [C] and moves every session. "We don't take Section 8" is lawful in some jurisdictions and actionable in others. **The single most commonly violated rule by out-of-state investors.**
- **[B] A blanket criminal-record ban invites disparate-impact liability.** HUD expects individualised assessment — nature and severity of the offence, how long ago, whether there was an actual conviction (an arrest alone is never a valid basis), and relevance to tenancy. Oakland, Berkeley, Seattle, Cook County and Detroit restrict or prohibit criminal screening outright.
- **[A] FCRA reaches tenant screening — the same statute Sensei already teaches for your own file.** Permissible purpose, written authorisation, and an **adverse action notice** if you deny, raise a deposit or require a cosigner based in whole or in part on a consumer report. *15 U.S.C. 1681b, 1681m.* **Write your screening criteria down, date them, and apply them mechanically to everyone.** Fair housing cases are decided on whether you can show the same standard was applied to all — the cheapest exposure in this whole domain to eliminate.
- **[A] Only three states cap rent statewide.** Oregon SB 608 — 7% plus CPI, maximum 10%. California AB 1482 — 5% plus regional CPI, maximum 10%; **6.3% for Aug 2025–Jul 2026, rising to 8.8% for Aug 2026–Jul 2027**, which reflects a high CPI print and should not be extrapolated. Washington HB 1217, signed 7 May 2025 — 7% plus CPI or 10%, whichever is lower, no increase in the first twelve months of tenancy, **manufactured-home lots capped at 5% with no expiry**, new construction exempt twelve years, residential caps sunsetting after fifteen. **Roughly thirty states preempt local rent control entirely. In a capped state, model rent growth at the statutory cap, not at market — your terminal value depends on a legislature.**
- **[C] Short-term rental regulation is the highest-velocity risk in real estate, and it ratchets one direction.** NYC Local Law 18 is effective prohibition — legal listings fell from roughly 22,000 to about 3,000 after September 2023 enforcement. Los Angeles caps a standard permit at **120 nights a year** and as of 2026 also requires an LAPD police permit with a host background check. Texas, Arizona and — new in 2026 — Idaho and Indiana preempt local bans. Dallas's 2023 single-family-zone ban is enjoined and unenforced, so **verify enforcement posture, not just code text.** The constituency for restriction is local and durable; the constituency for permission is diffuse, often non-resident and unpopular. **Underwrite every short-term rental twice — once at STR revenue, once at long-term rent. If it fails the second, you are buying a revocable licence, not real estate.**
- **[A] Wholesaling's "I'm selling a contract, not property" theory is losing, state by state.** Illinois limits an unlicensed wholesaler to **one transaction per twelve months**. Oklahoma's Predatory Real Estate Wholesaler Prohibition Act requires a licence to publicly market an equitable interest. South Carolina prohibits advertising property you do not own. Pennsylvania requires registration and profit disclosure. North Carolina, Virginia, Nebraska, Kentucky, Oregon and Washington impose significant requirements. **The consistent legal theory is that marketing the property rather than the contract is brokerage activity.** The compliant paths that remain everywhere: close it yourself and resell, get licensed, or assign privately with full written disclosure where permitted.
- **[A] Rental arbitrage needs two conditions in writing, and most operators have neither** — a lease expressly permitting subletting for the intended use, and local law permitting non-owner-occupied short-term rentals. **Structurally it is an operating business, not a real estate investment:** no equity, no depreciation, no appreciation, no amortisation, no 1031, one hundred percent of the operating risk, a fixed twelve-month obligation, and a revocable licence underneath. Evaluate it with the multiple you would apply to any small service business. **The version that is genuinely under-used is the commercial master lease with a fixed-price purchase option** — same idea, real asset, and an exit.
- **[A] Domestic entities are currently exempt from Corporate Transparency Act beneficial ownership reporting.** FinCEN's interim final rule of **21 March 2025** narrowed "reporting company" to entities formed under foreign law and registered to do business in a US state, removing over 99% of previously covered entities. A final rule has been expected during 2026 — confirm before relying on the exemption. *(As of July 2026; not re-checked.)*
- **Never hold appreciating real estate in an S or C corporation.** You lose the ability to distribute appreciated property without triggering gain, and you lose the 1014 step-up on the underlying asset. Real estate belongs in partnerships and disregarded entities. Flipping, brokerage and property management are where the S-corp election earns its keep.
- **[A] Raising money from passive investors to buy a building is a securities offering.** The 506(b) and 506(c) rules Sensei already teaches apply without modification — and syndicators breach them more often than software founders do, because "it's just a JV with a few friends" feels different when there is a building involved. It is not different.

## Real property — what a building is worth, and what an improvement is worth

- **The structural break is between unit four and unit five.** One to four units are valued by **sales comparison** — income is largely irrelevant and **you cannot force appreciation by raising rent.** Five units and above, and all commercial, are valued by **income capitalisation**, where operational improvement converts directly into value. This is the largest discontinuity in the asset class, and it is the honest argument for graduating out of houses.
- **[B] USPAP requires an appraiser to consider all three approaches, apply those necessary for a credible result, explain and support any approach omitted, and reconcile with stated weights.** The cost approach's under-used function is as a **ceiling check** — if you can build it new for less than the asking price, either the building is mispriced or you are missing something.
- **Value equals NOI divided by cap rate, so a change in value equals the change in NOI divided by the cap rate.** At a 6% cap every permanent dollar of annual NOI is **$16.67** of value; at 5%, $20.00; at 8%, $12.50. Forty units, an $85 monthly rent increase net of 6% vacancy, plus $9,000 of eliminated water expense, is $47,352 of NOI and **$789,200 of value at a 6% cap.** That is not a trick. It is what capitalising an income stream does, and it is why the same money spent on a single-family house creates at best what it cost.
- **You capture only the NOI a buyer's underwriter will credit.** Deferred maintenance, one-time income and above-market rents that generate turnover are all stripped out at exit. Durable, in order: below-market rents brought to market · structural expense reduction (tax appeal, insurance re-shop, submetering) · new income lines · occupancy, which is partly cyclical and gets normalised away.
- **[C] The six ways an offering memorandum lies, in order of frequency:** no capital reserve · no management fee where the seller self-manages · vacancy underwritten below market · trailing-three annualised instead of trailing-twelve · pro forma rents presented as current · **property tax not reforecast at your purchase price.** The last has ended more first commercial acquisitions than the other five together.
- **[C] Underwriting floors that survive scrutiny:** reserves $250–$400 per unit per year · management 3–8% of effective gross income even when self-managing · **exit cap at entry cap plus 50 to 100 basis points** · rehab contingency 15% cosmetic and 20–25% structural or pre-1980. **[C] The 50% rule** — operating expenses around half of gross rent — is defensible mainly as a check on any pro forma showing a 25% expense ratio, which is almost always missing capex, management or vacancy.
- **[B] Unpermitted finished space cannot be counted in gross living area or room count, and below-grade space is below-grade regardless of finish.** A three-bed one-bath house with two beautiful unpermitted baths is a three-one. **Retroactive permitting is frequently the highest-return line in a rehab budget** — it converts square footage from invisible to countable. *Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-1.3-05; ANSI Z765*
- **Appraisers estimate contributory value, not cost.** A $90,000 pool in a market that pays $25,000 for pools is $65,000 of consumption. Improvements above neighbourhood standard produce zero or negative contributory value — superadequacy. **The comps are the ceiling. Pull them before you set the budget, not after you have spent it.**
- **[C] as documentation, [D] as a causal claim — the renovation ROI data.** Zonda's Cost vs. Value Report (38 years, 150 metros) is the only serious dataset, and the most recent published edition is the **38th annual, released September 2025**. It is C for what real estate professionals *estimate* and D as a claim that the project *caused* the value, because the outcome is agent opinion rather than matched transactions. What it says: garage door replacement about **268%** ($4,672 to $12,526), steel entry door about **216%**, manufactured stone veneer about **206%**; **eight of the top ten projects are exterior replacements**; ADU construction about **41%** ($166,406 to $68,656); basement remodel about **71%**; **solar the second-lowest of everything measured** (Pacific 40.7%, New England 24.6%). Costs include a standard 10% materials and 10% labour markup, so self-performing raises your real ratio above the published one. **[F] Any citation to a "2026 Cost vs. Value Report" is unverified** — several widely-circulated pages quote the 2024 garage-door figure of 194% and label it 2026.
- **[D] "An ADU adds 20–30% to your property value" is a selection effect.** FHFA data showing California properties with ADUs at a median appraised value of $1,064,000 against $715,000 without is real, but ADUs cluster in high-value coastal California markets with large lots and permissive zoning. Zonda's controlled measure of marginal contribution is about 41% of cost. **Build an ADU for rent — $1,800 a month on $166,406 is roughly a 13% gross yield on cost, a far better argument — or for a duplex conversion. Not for resale.**
- **[C] 2026 construction costs:** about **$162 per square foot** nationally, up 7.3% on the 2024 NAHB baseline; production and semi-custom $175–$320; custom $300–$600 and above. Framing lumber up 18.2% from January 2024 to January 2026. Canadian softwood duty 14.54%; Section 232 duties of 50% on steel and aluminium, adding an estimated 8–12% on steel-intensive projects. **Existing assets trading below replacement cost carry a hard supply floor** — nobody builds competing product until values recover to cost, which is the strongest argument for acquisition in an otherwise negatively levered market. Budget a materials-inflation allowance on top of contingency; 2023–2024 rehab budgets are running 10–20% light.
- **Match the material specification to the hold period.** Metal roofing costs roughly 1.7–1.9 times asphalt and lasts two to three times as long — but **appraisers give minimal contributory value for a longer-lived roof**, because functional is functional in the sales comparison approach. Selling in five years: the cheapest roof that appraises and passes inspection. Holding twenty: metal. **Over-specifying on a short hold is charity to the next owner; under-specifying on a long hold is a loan from yourself at a punishing rate.** The exception worth paying for is insurance-driven — impact-rated roofing in hail states, Class A assemblies in wildfire zones — because on income property a permanent premium reduction capitalises at the full NOI multiplier.
- **[C] Land due diligence has four items that are cheap to check and ruinous to skip:** a chain of title run to the original patent for **severed minerals** (the mineral owner's rights are dominant over yours, and a standard 50–60 year search will not find a 1920s reservation) · **water rights**, which in prior-appropriation states are severable property that may not travel with the land · a **perc test**, often the single highest-return few hundred dollars in rural land because it removes the buyer's main uncertainty · and **agricultural or greenbelt rollback tax**, which recaptures the difference between use-value and market-value assessment for a lookback of commonly three to ten years plus interest the moment you convert to a non-qualifying use. Quantify the rollback before you buy, not at closing.

## Demand — what advertising and marketing actually do

- **[A] Mean short-term advertising elasticity is 0.12; long-term 0.24.** A 1% increase in advertising spend buys about **0.12%** more sales in the short run. To move sales 10% short-term on advertising alone, the average brand roughly doubles its budget. Elasticity runs higher for durables, early life-cycle, and yearly-measured data. *Sethuraman, Tellis & Briesch, Journal of Marketing Research 48(3), 2011 — meta-analysis of 751 short-term and 402 long-term elasticities across 56 studies.* **Any pitch promising an order of magnitude more is claiming the far tail of a well-measured distribution. Make them show why.**
- **[A] Most firms cannot measure advertising ROI at all.** Across 25 large digital field experiments totalling $2.8M in spend, the **median ROI confidence interval was over 100 percentage points wide**; individual sales carry a coefficient of variation near 10; an informative experiment can require **over 10 million person-weeks**. *Lewis & Rao, Quarterly Journal of Economics 130(4), 2015.* **This is not a reason to despair — it is a reason to stop reporting attributed ROAS as if it were ROI, and to plan on prior evidence instead.**
- **[A] Branded search ads are frequently not incremental.** In randomized experiments at eBay, brand-keyword ads produced **no measurable short-term benefit — about 99.5% of the attributed traffic would have arrived anyway** — and non-brand search returned negative average returns because unaffected frequent users consumed most of the spend. *Blake, Nosko & Tadelis, Econometrica 83(1), 2015.* Boundary: eBay had enormous awareness and strong organic presence. This does not automatically generalise to a small brand — **but it moves the burden of proof onto the spend.**
- **[A] Attribution is not measurement.** Across 15 Facebook RCTs — 500 million user-experiment observations, 1.6 billion impressions — observational methods **frequently failed to recover the experimental effect even with far richer data than any advertiser has.** *Gordon, Zettelmeyer, Bhargava & Chapsky, Marketing Science 38(2), 2019.* Platform-reported conversions and multi-touch attribution are allocation heuristics. Only randomized holdouts, geo-experiments and **ghost-ad designs** produce a defensible incrementality claim. *Johnson, Lewis & Nubbemeyer, JMR 54(6), 2017.*
- **[B] Brand growth comes from more buyers, not more loyalty.** Double jeopardy — smaller brands have both fewer buyers and slightly lower loyalty — replicates across 50+ categories and 30+ countries, including B2B. Loyalty is largely a **consequence** of size. *Ehrenberg, Goodhardt & Barwise, Journal of Marketing, 1990, and subsequent replication.* Caveat honestly: this describes how growth has historically decomposed; it does not by itself settle whether retention spend has better marginal return **in a subscription business with negative churn**.
- **[A] Word of mouth outperforms advertising per unit of input.** eWOM **volume elasticity 0.236, valence elasticity 0.417** — the latter more than triple the advertising elasticity. Higher for privately-consumed, low-trialability goods, and **higher on independent review sites than on retailer-owned ones**. *You, Vadakkepatt & Joshi, Journal of Marketing 79(2), 2015.*
- **[A] A one-star review increase causes a 5–9% revenue increase — for independents. Zero effect for chains.** Regression discontinuity on Yelp rounding thresholds, outcome measured from **state tax records**, not self-report. *Luca, HBS Working Paper 12-016 (2011, rev. 2016) — a working paper, not a peer-reviewed article; the design is what earns the A.* **The asymmetry is the strategic content: reviews substitute for brand information.** If you are unknown, review generation may be your highest-return activity. Once you have recognition, it is hygiene.
- **[A] Review manipulation is detectable and concentrates exactly where the incentive is strongest** — independents facing nearby competitors. *Mayzlin, Dover & Chevalier, American Economic Review 104(8), 2014.* Your competitors' ratings may be inflated; following them is how listings get removed.
- **[A] NPS is not a validated growth predictor.** Tested directly across 21 firms and 15,500+ interviews — including in the industries cited as NPS exemplars — the superiority and best-predictor claims **failed to replicate**. *Keiningham, Cooil, Andreassen & Aksoy, Journal of Marketing 71(3), 2007.* It is a cheap tracking metric. It is not a justification for a decision.
- **[A] Disclosing influencer sponsorship raises recall, costs credibility, and has statistically insignificant net effect on behaviour.** *Meta-analysis, Marketing Letters — 288 effect sizes, 37 studies, n=12,721.* Note honestly that a separate meta-analysis reports a negative disclosure-to-purchase-intention correlation; the field is not unanimous. **Either way, the trade you think you are making by under-disclosing is not supported — and FTC 16 CFR Part 255 (effective 26 July 2023) requires disclosure that is clear, conspicuous, unavoidable in interactive media, and matched to format.**
- **[C] SEO: what is documented and what is not.** Core Web Vitals thresholds are published exactly — LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, CLS ≤0.1, at the **75th percentile** over a **28-day** CrUX window. Google's local model is exactly three documented factors — **relevance, distance, prominence** — with **no published weights**, and ranking cannot be purchased. **Distance is not optimisable**; it is a hard bound on a local business's market and should bound its budget. **[F] "Google has 200 ranking factors"** originated as an offhand estimate around 2009 and was never a published list.
- **[B] AI search is displacing informational clicks, but not by the number people quote.** Pew Research, tracking **900 US adults across 68,879 Google searches** (results collected 7–17 April 2025): users clicked a result on **8%** of visits with an AI summary versus **15%** without, and clicked a source *inside* the summary on about **1%**. **This is not causal** — Google chooses which queries get summaries and favours simple informational ones that already ended without clicks. Vendor figures of 34% or 58% come from undisclosed panels. **What survives: content whose only value is answering a question a model can answer is a depreciating asset.**
- **[B] Generative engine optimisation has exactly one peer-reviewed evidence base.** On a ~10,000-query benchmark, the best methods improved visibility by roughly **41%** on position-adjusted word count and **28%** on subjective impression. The winners were **citing sources, adding quotations, adding statistics**. **Keyword stuffing was among the least effective.** *Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024.* It measures visibility inside generated answers, not traffic or revenue, and it is model-version dependent. **[F] Any "GEO ranking factor" list with weights is fabricated — no model provider publishes citation criteria.**

## Demand — social programs

- **[A] Passive broadcast beats active sharing in aggregate.** Randomized field experiment across **1.4 million friends of 9,687 Facebook users**: passive-broadcast features (automatic visible signal from ordinary use) drove a **246% increase in peer influence and contagion**; active-personalized sharing added only **98%** more and was used far less often, so **passive generated more total adoption**. *Aral & Walker, Management Science 57(9), 2011, 1623–1639.* **Most companies build share buttons — the weaker half of the mechanism. This is a product decision, not a marketing one, and it cannot be retrofitted cheaply.**
- **[A] Weak ties do the heavy lifting.** Randomizing exposure to friends' sharing across **253 million subjects**: exposure causally increased sharing and accelerated it; strong ties are individually more influential, but **weak ties are far more numerous and collectively drive most novel information spread**. *Bakshy, Rosenn, Marlow & Adamic, WWW 2012.* Breadth beats depth when the objective is diffusion.
- **[B] Virality is a distribution, not a strategy.** Across **seven independent online domains**, the vast majority of cascades are small and **terminate within one generation of the seed**. *Goel, Watts & Goldstein, ACM EC 2012; Goel et al., Management Science 2016.* **A plan requiring an outlier draw is not a plan.** The useful corollary: because most diffusion is broadcast-shaped, **initial seed audience size dominates outcomes** far more than any content property. Reach is controllable; depth is not.
- **[B] High-arousal emotion travels; low-arousal does not.** Awe, anger and anxiety increase sharing; sadness decreases it — controlling for surprise, interest, utility and placement. *Berger & Milkman, JMR 49(2), 2012, on the full NYT corpus over three months.* Boundary: this measures sharing of **news articles**, not sales of anything.
- **[A] Early positive signal causally shapes later aggregate signal — asymmetrically.** A single arbitrary first upvote raised final ratings by about **25%** and raised the odds of a subsequent upvote by about **32%**; negative first votes produced **no symmetric effect** — crowds corrected downward manipulation but not upward. *Muchnik, Aral & Taylor, Science 341, 2013, across 100,000+ posts.* **The measured outcome is a rating, not revenue. Seeding your own launch legitimately is sound; reciprocal engagement rings move a metric with no revenue evidence and violate every major platform's terms.**
- **[F] There is no credible evidence on optimal posting time, frequency, hashtag count, or video length.** Every circulating figure is a vendor analysing its own customers. Test on your own account if it matters; expect a small effect; do not pay for someone else's answer.
- **[F] "The algorithm rewards X" is unfalsifiable.** No major social platform publishes ranking criteria. That does not make every such claim false — it makes none of them checkable.
- **[B] Follower count is a lagging indicator, not an asset.** Double jeopardy predicts large brands have more of everything, followers included. Closing a follower gap does not close a share gap.

## Sizing a market without buying a research report

Bottom-up beats top-down. A top-down estimate — "the market is $X billion, we take 1%" — contains no information; the 1% is invented.

```
Addressable units × Purchase frequency × Realistic price × Achievable penetration
```

Every term must trace to a named source or a stated, falsifiable assumption. **[C] The free primary sources:** Census **County Business Patterns** (establishments by NAICS by county — the B2B universe), Census **Economic Census** (revenue by industry, every five years), Census **Business Formation Statistics**, Census **ACS** (tract-level demographics for local radius work), BLS **Consumer Expenditure Survey** (household spending by category and demographic), BLS **Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics** (buyer-population sizing by role and metro), and **SEC EDGAR** for audited competitor figures.

**[C] Google Trends is a normalised 0–100 index from a sampled subset — not absolute volume.** Separately-run queries are not comparable; compare only within one simultaneous query. Low-volume terms are unstable. A systematic review found only ~7% of published Trends analyses were reproducible.

**Before spending on a "trend," name two independent non-attention data series that should move if it is real** — category revenue in the Economic Census, employment in the relevant NAICS code, public-company segment reporting. **If the only evidence is attention data, you have found attention, not a market.**

## Structure and law

- **Piercing the veil** turns on totality: commingling (the heavyweight), undercapitalization, failure to observe formalities, alter ego, misleading third parties, and whether respecting the entity would sanction fraud. Sign as *[Name], Managing Member, [Company] LLC*.
- **Transferring assets after a claim arises is a fraudulent transfer** — unwindable, and it supplies evidence of intent.
- **Asset protection is layered:** insurance first, then separation (operating co / holding co / IP entity / equipment entity) — which only holds with written intercompany agreements at arm's length with payments actually made — then charging-order protection, which varies by state and is weaker for single-member LLCs.
- **QSBS (§1202) requires a C corporation.** Stock acquired after 4 July 2025: 50% exclusion at 3 years, 75% at 4, 100% at 5+; per-issuer cap **$15M**; gross asset ceiling **$75M**. Most professional services, financial services, hospitality and farming excluded.

## Payments

- **[A] Regulation E protects consumer accounts — business accounts get almost none of it.** No statutory error-resolution clock, no capped liability, no provisional credit right. The business gets its deposit agreement plus UCC Article 4A for wires, which allocates loss to the customer when the bank followed a commercially reasonable security procedure. Same asymmetry as personal versus business credit reporting. *15 U.S.C. §1693a(2); 12 CFR §1005.2(b), §1005.3; UCC 4A-202*
- **[A] Reg E error resolution runs 60 / 10 / 45.** Consumer must notify within **60 days of the statement** on which the error first appears. Institution then has **10 business days** to investigate or must provisionally credit and take up to **45 days**. New accounts, POS debit and foreign-initiated transfers extend to 20 business days and 90 days. *12 CFR §1005.11*
- **[A] Unauthorized-transfer liability is tiered by reporting speed:** $50 within two business days of learning of the loss; $500 if later but within 60 days of the statement; **unlimited** for transfers after a missed 60-day window. Network zero-liability policies are contractual promises, not statutory rights. *12 CFR §1005.6*
- **[C] A chargeback is a network rule, not a federal right.** Visa/Mastercard/Amex/Discover operating rules — private contracts, network timelines, changeable at will. Federal rights are separate and narrower: Reg E for unauthorized EFTs, Reg Z §1666 billing error and §1666i claims-and-defenses on credit cards.
- **[A] Debit interchange cap is 21 cents + 5 basis points + 1 cent fraud adjustment.** Issuers under **$10 billion** in assets are statutorily exempt — which is why community banks and credit unions earn materially more per debit transaction. *15 U.S.C. §1693o-2; 12 CFR Part 235*
- **[D] The interchange standard is in genuine legal limbo.** The Fed's 2023 proposal to cut the cap to 14.4 cents was never finalised. In *Corner Post v. Board of Governors* a North Dakota federal court **vacated** Reg II's interchange standard in 2025 and then **stayed the vacatur pending appeal** to the Eighth Circuit, so the cap still applies while the appeal runs; a separate challenge, *Linney's Pizza*, is on appeal in the Sixth Circuit. *(As of 1 October 2026.)* Do not model debit economics on a fixed number without flagging the challenge.
- **[A] The CFPB overdraft fee cap was repealed and cannot simply be reissued.** The December 2024 rule capping overdraft at $5 was nullified by a Congressional Review Act joint resolution signed **9 May 2025**. It never took effect, and the CRA bars a substantially similar rule without new legislation. Any advice premised on a $5 cap describes a rule that does not exist.
- **[B] FedNow raised its transaction limit from $1 million to $10 million** effective November 2025; participation passed ~1,700 institutions by April 2026. Individual institutions may set lower limits.
- **[B] Same Day ACH per-entry limit rising to $10 million**, and Nacha fraud-monitoring rules take effect **19 June 2026** (a federal holiday — practical date is the next banking day). Note: Nacha is a private association. Its rules bind by contract, not statute.
- **[A] A payment stablecoin is not a deposit and is not insured.** Under the GENIUS Act a permitted issuer owes reserve and redemption duties; the holder's claim runs against that issuer's balance sheet. No FDIC or NCUA coverage, no Reg E error-resolution clock, no provisional credit. **Price it as unsecured counterparty exposure, because that is what it is.**
- **[C] Stablecoin settlement is effectively irrevocable**, like the instant rails below. No chargeback mechanism, no network arbitration, recovery dependent on cooperation rather than right. **If you accept it you have traded chargeback exposure for irreversibility. That may be the better trade — make it deliberately.**
- **[A] Basel's cryptoasset prudential standard (SCO60) is in force in the Basel Framework from 1 January 2026 — and binds no bank until its own regulator implements it.** Industry associations sought recalibration, the Committee ran a targeted review during 2026, and Singapore's MAS consulted on deferring to 2027 or later *(as of July 2026)*. **This is the standard-versus-law distinction in live form.** Your bank's appetite is set by its national regulator, never by Basel.
- **[C] Instant payments are effectively irrevocable.** No chargeback mechanism; recovery depends on the receiving institution's cooperation, not a right. Critical for authorised push-payment fraud: when a consumer is deceived into sending money themselves, the transfer is technically authorised and Reg E's unauthorised-transfer protections do not squarely reach it. Treat instant rails as cash.

## Deposits

- **[A] Deposit insurance is $250,000 per depositor, per institution, per ownership category — not per account.** Single, joint, certain retirement and trust accounts are separate categories, so one person at one bank can be insured well above $250,000. Three single-ownership checking accounts at the same bank add nothing. *12 U.S.C. §1821(a); 12 U.S.C. §1787(k); 12 CFR Parts 330, 745*
- **[B] Revocable trust coverage caps at $1,250,000 per owner per institution** — $250,000 per eligible beneficiary, capped at five. Graded B because secondary sources disagree on the current rule's effective date; the arithmetic is consistent, the date is not.
- **[A] Check-hold thresholds rose 1 July 2025:** next-business-day availability of the first **$275** (from $225); large-deposit and new-account thresholds **$6,725** (from $5,525). Adjusted every five years by CPI-W — which is exactly how stale numbers survive in circulation. *12 CFR Part 229*
- **[A] A credit union is a member-owned cooperative.** Same $250,000 coverage through the NCUA Share Insurance Fund. Surplus returns to members as better rates and lower fees rather than to shareholders — and sub-$10B institutions keep uncapped debit interchange, which helps fund that pricing.

## Consumer protection and benefits access

- **[B] The CFPB now says employer-partnered earned wage access is not credit under TILA.** Advisory opinion published **23 December 2025**; formally rescinds the 2024 proposed interpretive rule. "Covered EWA" requires four features: based on accrued wages using actual payroll data; repaid by payroll deduction; **no recourse** against the worker if deductions fall short; and **no credit-risk assessment**. Direct-to-consumer EWA failing any of these is outside the opinion.
- **[C] State law on EWA diverges sharply from the federal position.** New York sued two providers in April 2025 arguing ~$5 per-advance fees equal 250–750% effective APR. Several states have EWA statutes covering licensing, fee caps and non-recourse requirements. A product lawful under the CFPB opinion can still be an unlicensed loan in a given state.
- **[A] 2026 maximum EITC is $8,231** with three or more qualifying children (from $8,046). Investment income above **$12,200** disqualifies outright (it was $11,950 for 2025). Refundable, and heavily audited — substantiation of the qualifying-child relationship and residency tests matters more than usual. *IRC §32; Rev. Proc. 2025-32, read directly 1 October 2026.*
- **[B] FY2026 SNAP maximum allotments** (48 states + DC), effective 1 October 2025: one person **$298**, two $546, three $785, four **$994**, five $1,183, six $1,421. Allotments and eligibility standards adjust every 1 October.
- **[B] CDFI Fund FY2026 rounds:** Bank Enterprise Award Program up to **$40 million** for eligible FDIC-insured depositories; Small Dollar Loan Program up to **$9 million**. Flows to certified institutions, not to operating businesses — the distinction most "federal money" pitches blur.

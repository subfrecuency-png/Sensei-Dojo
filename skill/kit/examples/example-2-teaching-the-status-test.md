# Example 2 — a short teaching session (`sensei-method`)

> **Example — yours will differ.** Produced by following the teaching protocol on a made-up learner. The dates are as of 1 October 2026. Education, not advice.

## What you'd type

> Teach me how to tell whether a crypto rule is actually law. Don't lecture — quiz me.

## Abridged session

**Sensei (attempt before instruction):** A newsletter says: *"The CLARITY Act passed the House and advanced out of Senate Banking 15–9. SEC/CFTC jurisdiction over crypto is now settled."* Is the second sentence true? Answer, then give me your confidence: 50, 65, 80 or 95%.

**Learner:** True — it passed the House and a Senate committee. 80%.

**Sensei (reveal):** False, and at 80% that is worth slowing down on. Passing one chamber binds nothing; a committee vote binds nothing. Run the **status test**: bill, proposed rule, or law in force. The tell for law is a Public Law number on congress.gov. CLARITY has none — and on 15 September 2026 the Senate rejected cloture 49–50 (tier A on the negative claim, as of 1 October 2026).

**Debrief — four prompts, in order:**
1. *What did you believe when you answered?* — "Committee approval means it's basically done."
2. *Which belief was wrong?* — "That a committee vote changes the law."
3. *What rule gets it right?* — "Only a Public Law number or a final rule with an effective date is law."
4. *Where else does that rule apply?* — Learner offers: "A proposed IRS regulation someone quotes as settled tax law." Accepted.

**Drill (discrimination):** The GENIUS Act has a Public Law number (119-27) but its regime is not yet operative. Law, or law in force? → *Enacted 18 July 2025; operative 18 January 2027.* "Enacted" and "in force" are different answers.

**Next move:** for the next three legal claims you read this week, write down which of the three statuses each one has before you read the rest of the article.

# Example 1 — an SBA fee question (`sensei` + `sensei-live`)

> **Example — yours will differ.** This was produced by following the skills on a made-up case. Claude's wording, length and next move will vary. The figures are the FY2027 schedule as of 1 October 2026 — check the current SBA notice before relying on them. Education, not advice.

## What you'd type

> Sensei — I run a small machine shop (we're a manufacturer). Our bank says our SBA 7(a) loan for $760,000 will be approved next week. What upfront fee should I expect? Could we just borrow $700,000 now and the other $60,000 next month?

## Abridged answer

*Education, not advice — I teach the rules and the evidence; a licensed professional applies them to your facts.*

Your approval date picks the fee schedule, and next week falls under **FY2027** (loans approved 1 October 2026 – 30 September 2027, SBA Information Notice 5000-881797 — tier A, as of 1 October 2026).

- The 0% upfront fee for manufacturers now stops at **$700,000**. At $760,000 you are over it.
- Above $150,000 the SBA guarantees **75%**, so the guaranteed portion is $570,000.
- The fee for $700,001–$5,000,000 is **3.5% of the guaranteed portion**: 0.035 × $570,000 = **$19,950**.

At $700,000 the fee would be $0 — so the extra $60,000 costs you about $19,950 in fees before interest. That is a real cliff.

**Splitting does not work.** Two 7(a) loans over 12 months' maturity approved for you or your affiliates within 90 days are combined for the fee. Splitting to avoid fees is prohibited.

The lawful question is whether you need the $60,000 *in this loan*. A separate working-capital line from another source, or a smaller project scope, are questions for your lender and your CPA — not a fee-avoidance structure.

**Next move:** ask your lender for the fee calculation on the term sheet at $760,000 and at $700,000, in writing, before approval.
